Standard Thickness Target (15–25 µm) — up to 25–50 µm for High-Reliability
The copper layer is the workhorse of this stack. Standard applications require 15–25 µm (the figure shown); high-reliability or high-thermal-cycling applications may require 25–50 µm. At 3 A/dm² with near-theoretical efficiency the plating rate is approximately 1.2–1.3 µm/min, so a 15–25 µm build takes roughly 12–20 minutes. Calculate your plating time based on the thickness target and measured plating rate for your bath.

Why Copper Thickness Matters
The copper layer serves dual functions: electrical conductivity and mechanical stress absorption. Aluminum and silver have different coefficients of thermal expansion. During thermal cycling, the interface between them experiences significant shear stress. The ductile copper interlayer absorbs this stress, preventing cracking and delamination. Insufficient copper thickness leads to premature failure under thermal cycling. Maintain carbonate below 60 g/L — high carbonate reduces throwing power and deposit quality.

Anode Management

Use OFHC (Oxygen-Free High Conductivity) copper anodes in polypropylene or Dynel bags. The bags trap anode sludge/particles and keep them out of solution, preventing rough deposits. Maintain anode-to-cathode area ratio between 2:1 and 1:1. Dissolving copper anodes replenish the bath — unlike the strike bath which uses inert steel anodes.

A:C ratio 2:1 to 1:1. Replace anode bags when torn or clogged.

Carbonate Control

Sodium carbonate builds continuously from CO₂ absorption (accelerated at high temperature) and cyanide oxidation. Keep below 60 g/L. High carbonate reduces conductivity, current efficiency, and throwing power. Remove by cooling the solution to crystallize Na₂CO₃ — filter and discard the crystals.

Na₂CO₃ <60 g/L. Freezing crystallization at <0°C is standard removal method.

Troubleshooting Copper plate issues

Thin or Uneven Deposit

Low copper concentration, insufficient agitation, or poor current distribution. Check Cu metal level. Verify anode condition and A:C ratio. Improve agitation — air or cathode rod movement.

Rough or Nodular Deposit

Particulate contamination from anode bags (torn), high carbonate, or organic contamination. Check anode bags. Carbon-treat solution if organics suspected. Check carbonate level.

Dark or Burned High-CD Areas

Current density too high, free cyanide too low, or temperature too low. Reduce current or increase free NaCN. Verify bath temperature is 60–71°C.

Low Current Efficiency

Free cyanide too high, carbonate excessive, or temperature too low. Optimize free NaCN to 8–15 g/L. Remove carbonate. Increase temperature to target range.

⚠ Safety
CYANIDE BATH operating at elevated temperature (140–160°F). High temperature increases HCN vapor pressure — ventilation is critical. Maintain slot ventilation at minimum 100 fpm face velocity. Never mix with acid — generates lethal HCN gas. Maintain pH >10. HCN monitors must be calibrated and operational. Cyanide antidote kit accessible within 30 seconds. Thermal burns are an additional hazard at this temperature — wear heat-resistant chemical gloves. Know emergency shower and eyewash locations.

Double Zincate — The Adhesion Reliability Layer

The second zincate is thinner but dramatically more uniform than the first. This is what gives the double-zincate process its adhesion reliability. Single zincate adhesion is inconsistent — this step is mandatory for critical aluminum plating.

Thinner Than First, More Uniform — This Is the Adhesion Layer
The second zincate deposit is approximately half the thickness of the first (0.10–0.25 vs. 0.25–0.50 µm) but has dramatically finer grain structure and better uniformity. Immersion time is shorter (15–30 seconds vs. 30–60). The first zincate conditioned the surface; the second provides the actual adhesion foundation for all layers above.

First Zincate

Immersion 30–60 seconds. Deposit thickness 0.25–0.50 µm. Appearance: medium-gray. Coarser crystal structure. Purpose: condition the aluminum surface and remove residual oxide. Gets stripped off in the zincate strip.

Thicker deposit, coarser grain — conditioning layer only.

Second Zincate (This Step)

Immersion 15–30 seconds. Deposit thickness 0.10–0.25 µm. Appearance: very thin, uniform light-gray. Much finer crystal structure. Purpose: provide the actual adhesion foundation for copper and silver. This layer stays permanently.

Thinner deposit, finer grain — the real adhesion layer.
Why the Second Zincate Is Superior
The first zincate cycle (zincate, rinse, then strip) conditions the aluminum surface by displacing residual oxide and creating a microscopically uniform surface topography. When this first zinc layer is stripped, the exposed aluminum is far more receptive to zinc displacement than virgin aluminum. The second zincate nucleates more uniformly, with finer crystal size and fewer voids. This translates directly to better adhesion: the copper strike bonds to a continuous, fine-grained zinc interlayer rather than a coarse, potentially porous one.

Troubleshooting Second zincate issues

Deposit Looks Like First Zincate

Over-immersion (too long in tank). Second zincate should be lighter and thinner than the first. Reduce time toward 15 seconds. If the deposit is as thick as the first, the surface conditioning from the strip cycle is being negated.

Bare Areas or Incomplete Coverage

Oxide reformed between the strip and this step. Parts likely air-dried during transfer. Verify transfer time from the preceding rinse is under 30 seconds with no air exposure. May need to re-strip and re-process from the zincate strip.

Dark Spots or Discoloration

Zincate bath contamination (iron, copper drag-in). Same troubleshooting as the first zincate. Carbon-treat the bath. Check upstream rinse integrity. Also verify the post-strip rinse is fully removing nitric-strip acid — incomplete rinsing lets acid carry into the zincate and depress local pH.

Copper or Silver Peels Downstream

Second zincate adhesion failure. Verify double-zincate was actually performed (not single). Check that the zincate strip was complete. Confirm second zincate was thin and uniform. Verify hot entry on copper strike.

⚠ Safety
Zincate solution is strongly alkaline (NaOH 120–150 g/L, pH >13). Causes severe chemical burns on contact. Wear face shield, chemical-resistant gloves, apron, and rubber boots. If solution contacts skin, flush immediately with water for 15 minutes. Eye contact is a medical emergency — flush with water and seek immediate medical attention. Maintain slot ventilation on the zincate tank.

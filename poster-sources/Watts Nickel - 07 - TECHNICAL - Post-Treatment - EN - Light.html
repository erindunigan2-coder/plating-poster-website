H-Relief Bake
For high-strength steel, the most important post-treatment is the hydrogen-embrittlement relief bake — 375 ±25°F for at least 8 hours, started within 4 hours of the last plating step, per ASTM B850 (use current revision). This is a safety-critical step for load-bearing hardened parts, not an optional finishing touch.
This Is Not a Chrome Line
On a bright nickel line, post-treatment usually means decorative chromium. Plain Watts is engineering nickel — its post-treatment is more often a bake, a topcoat, or nothing at all. A chromium topcoat is one possible option among many, not the headline. Choose the treatment from the part’s end use: load-bearing hardened steel gets the bake; solderable and brazeable surfaces often ship as-plated; salvage build-up goes to the machine shop.

As-Plated & Storage

Plain Watts nickel is corrosion-resistant but not corrosion-proof — it slowly tarnishes and hazes in humid or industrial air. For parts stored before assembly or topcoating, keep them clean, dry, and protected (VCI packaging or desiccant). For a subsequent plating layer (chrome, gold), re-activate the nickel — it passivates on standing and needs a light acid dip to restore adhesion before the next deposit.

Final Rinse & Dry — Closing the Line

  • DI water rinse, ambient, gentle immersion 15–30 sec
  • Warm air dry: 120–140°F max
  • No cure time for the nickel itself; topcoats cure per their own TDS
  • Handle with clean, dry, lint-free gloves — fingerprints on fresh nickel can stain or interfere with topcoat adhesion
⚠ If a Chromium Topcoat Is Applied (Optional)
If the job calls for decorative or hard chromium over the Watts nickel, the chrome process carries its own severe hazards. Hexavalent chromium (Cr⁶⁺) is a confirmed human carcinogen (IARC Group 1). OSHA PEL: 5 µg/m³ TWA (action level 2.5 µg/m³) as Cr(VI). Full PPE, mist suppressant, local exhaust, and medical surveillance per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1026 apply. This is the chrome line’s responsibility — see the Decorative Chrome / Hard Chrome references — not the nickel line’s.

Troubleshooting Post-treatment issues

Nickel Tarnishes or Hazes in Storage

Cause: Expected — bare nickel is not corrosion-proof. Fix: Apply anti-tarnish dip or topcoat; use VCI packaging.

Topcoat (Chrome/Gold) Peels From Nickel

Cause: Nickel passivated on standing; not re-activated. Fix: Re-activate nickel with a light acid dip before topcoat; minimize delay.

Cracking After Bake on Thick Deposits

Cause: Deposit was already high-stress (contamination). Fix: Fix at the plating stage — analyze metals, lower CD. The bake relieves hydrogen, not deposit stress.

Stain / Fingerprints Under Topcoat

Cause: Handling with bare or dirty hands. Fix: Handle with clean lint-free gloves; keep parts clean and dry.

Build-Up Cracks During Machining

Cause: Excessive internal stress in the deposit. Fix: Fix at the plating stage (temp, pH, CD, contamination); sound plain Watts nickel should machine well.

Bake Timing Reminder

The 4-hour clock starts at completion of the last electrodeposition step. Some specifications require baking before any post-treatment or second plating layer — verify per customer spec. Testing per ASTM F519 where required.

⚠ Safety
Bake ovens are a burn and fire hazard — follow oven LOTO and hot-work procedures; no flammables near ovens. Anti-tarnish and seal chemistries: consult the specific product SDS. If a chromium topcoat is applied, full Cr(VI) controls apply per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1026 (see chrome references). Nickel remains a skin sensitizer through handling — gloves at all times.

µS/cm — Conductivity Target
Acid drag-in to an alkaline zinc bath neutralizes NaOH, drops the NaOH:Zn ratio, wastes chemistry, and degrades deposit quality. This rinse is your firewall.

Key Procedures 7 steps

1
Transfer from acid immediately. Parts must stay wet.
2
Immerse fully. Agitate vigorously — 5–8 vertical strokes minimum.
3
Hold 30–60 sec. Counter-current systems: enter the dirty stage first.
4
Double-rinse setups: repeat immersion and agitation in the second stage.
5
Check conductivity. If >200 µS/cm, increase flow or dump-and-refill.
6
Check pH. Below 5 = excessive acid drag-in. Investigate drain time.
7
Drain 15–20 sec before entering the plating bath.

Do best practice

  • Use two rinse stages here if at all possible
  • Monitor conductivity and pH every shift
  • Drain thoroughly — gravity is free
  • Replace rinse water aggressively at this station

Don’t avoid

  • Rely on a single static dip tank
  • Assume it’s fine because plating “seems okay”
  • Rush parts into plating dripping wet
  • Save water at the expense of plating bath contamination

Troubleshooting Common issues

Conductivity Consistently High

Acid drag-out too heavy or flow rate too low. Increase drain time from acid; increase rinse flow; add counter-current stage.

Plating Bath NaOH Dropping Fast

Acid drag-in through this rinse. Tighten conductivity target; add double rinse; titrate bath more frequently.

Chloride Buildup in Bath

HCl drag-in through rinse. Switch to DI rinse water; consider H₂SO₄ activation instead.

Parts Re-Oxidizing

Transfer too slow; parts drying. Speed up — parts must enter plating within 30 sec of leaving this rinse.

⚠ Safety
Dilute acid residue in rinse water. Mild skin irritation risk. Wet floors — leading cause of slip injuries on plating lines. PPE: chemical splash goggles, chemical-resistant gloves, non-slip footwear. Maintain housekeeping.

Best Practices Critical Guidelines

✓ Do

  • Confirm bake is complete before grinding
  • Use flood coolant at all times — non-negotiable
  • Take light passes (0.0005–0.002 in)
  • Measure dimensions frequently with calibrated instruments

✗ Don’t

  • NEVER dry grind hard chrome — Cr(VI) dust is carcinogenic
  • Use heavy infeed — heat checks and cracks the deposit
  • Grind from bare substrate into the chrome deposit—always grind from chrome outward toward the edge to prevent lifting
  • Let dry chrome swarf accumulate — wet-clean or HEPA-vacuum only

Heat Checking Mechanism & Prevention
Hard chrome at 850–1000 HV is extremely abrasion-resistant but brittle. When grinding generates localized surface temperatures above ~350°F, differential thermal expansion between the chrome deposit and the steel substrate creates tensile stress in the chrome layer. This produces a micro-crack network (heat checking) that is invisible to the naked eye but propagates rapidly under cyclic loading. The cracks also provide pathways for corrosion, defeating the purpose of the coating. Flood coolant is non-negotiable — dry grinding generates Cr(VI) particulate (OSHA PEL 5 µg/m³) and thermally damages the deposit. Maximum 0.001 in infeed per pass prevents thermal shock. Always grind from the deposit toward the edge, never from bare metal into the chrome, to prevent edge lifting and delamination. Dress the grinding wheel frequently — a loaded or glazed wheel generates more friction heat per unit of material removed.
⚠ Safety — Grinding & Final Finishing
Chrome grinding dust contains Cr(VI) — confirmed carcinogen. DRY GRINDING IS PROHIBITED. Flood coolant required at all times. Machine must have local exhaust ventilation and coolant containment. If any dust is visible, STOP and check coolant flow. P100 respiratory protection if Cr(VI) above OSHA PEL. Safety glasses + face shield for heavy grinding. Chrome swarf = hazardous waste (RCRA D007). Used grinding coolant = potentially hazardous. Cover open cuts before grinding.

Cyanide Zone Safety Barrier — No Acid Past This Point
This rinse separates the acid pretreatment zone (clean through the second zincate) from the cyanide plating zone (copper strike through silver plating). Acid contamination in any cyanide bath generates hydrogen cyanide gas (HCN) — lethal at 50 ppm. Conductivity must be below 50 µS/cm. This is not just a process parameter — it is a life-critical safety checkpoint.

What Gets Removed

Excess zincate solution (NaOH, dissolved zinc, Rochelle salt) from the second zincate. Also removes any residual acid contamination that may have been carried through the zincate bath from upstream steps. This rinse must produce water clean enough that no acid or alkaline residue enters the cyanide zone.

Alkaline-to-cyanide transition: Zincate is pH >13, Cu strike is mildly alkaline (pH ~11). The risk is not pH mismatch — it is trace acid from upstream reaching the cyanide baths.

The Cyanide Zone Boundary

The cyanide zone ahead holds four cyanide baths — the copper strike, copper plate, silver strike and silver plate (NaCN or KCN); the rinses between them are water rinses. Any acid that crosses this boundary — even trace quantities in rinse water dragout — reacts with cyanide to generate hydrogen cyanide gas (HCN). HCN is immediately dangerous to life at 50 ppm. This rinse is the last line of defense between the acid pretreatment zone and the cyanide plating zone.

ACID + CYANIDE = HCN GAS. This rinse prevents that reaction from ever happening.

Dual Purpose: Process and Safety
Every rinse on this line serves a process purpose, but this one also serves a critical safety function. The process requirement is removing zincate residue so the copper strike operates properly. The safety requirement is ensuring zero acid contamination enters the cyanide zone. Both must be satisfied before parts proceed. Additionally, the zinc layer on the parts is reactive — it will begin to oxidize in air, reducing adhesion if transfer to the copper strike is delayed. Speed and cleanliness are equally important here.

Troubleshooting Rinse station issues

Conductivity Above 50 µS/cm

Excessive zincate dragout or insufficient DI flow. Increase flow rate. Let parts drain longer over the zincate tank. Consider adding a third rinse stage. Do not transfer parts to the cyanide zone until conductivity is confirmed.

Copper Strike Adhesion Failure

Zinc layer oxidized during transfer. Reduce transfer time to under 30 seconds. Keep parts submerged until ready to move. Also verify that rinse water is clean — contamination in the rinse can deposit on the zinc and interfere with copper strike adhesion.

Zincate Residue on Parts

Visible white streaks or soapy feel on parts. Increase rinse time. Increase DI flow. Check that rinse tank is not overloaded with dragout — dump and refill if conductivity cannot be maintained. NaOH residue carried into the copper strike shifts bath chemistry.

HCN Alarm in Cyanide Zone

Acid contamination has reached a cyanide bath. EVACUATE immediately. Investigate source — likely acid carryover through this rinse station or through shared rinse lines. Verify dedicated rinse tanks with no cross-contamination. This is a life-threatening emergency.

⚠ Safety
This rinse is the safety barrier between the acid pretreatment zone and the cyanide plating zone. The four cyanide baths in the zone ahead are the copper strike, copper plate, silver strike and silver plate (the stages between them are water rinses). Acid contamination reaching any cyanide bath generates hydrogen cyanide gas (HCN), which is immediately dangerous to life at 50 ppm. Verify conductivity <50 µS/cm before transferring any parts past this point. NEVER allow acid solutions, acid-contaminated tools, or acid-contaminated rinse water to contact any cyanide tank or its dedicated rinse system. Rinse water from this station may contain alkaline zincate residue — wear chemical-resistant gloves.

This sequence determines whether your chrome sticks or peels. No shortcuts.

5 STEPS
Soak → Reverse Etch → Strike → Plate → Ramp Down
The etch-strike is the core skill of hard chrome plating. Master this sequence and your adhesion is reliable. Skip or rush it and parts peel.
⚠ Note: This etch-strike sequence is performed BEFORE sustained plating begins.

Best Practices Critical Guidelines

✓ Do

  • Switch polarity INSTANTLY from etch to strike — no dwell at zero
  • Ramp current smoothly — no interruptions
  • Verify fume suppression at full capacity during etch phase
  • Repeat full etch-strike if current is interrupted

✗ Don’t

  • Dwell at zero current between etch and strike — surface passivates instantly
  • Interrupt current during plating — adhesion fails at the interruption
  • Switch polarity while standing in puddles — use dry insulated matting
  • Simply restart current after a power failure — consult process engineer

Power Failure Recovery Protocol

During etch phase: Restart full etch-strike sequence from soak. The partially etched surface has re-passivated.
During strike phase: Restart full etch-strike. The thin chrome layer over re-passivated metal will peel.
During sustained plate (>5 min in): Consult process engineer. May be salvageable with a re-etch and re-strike at reduced parameters, but adhesion at the interruption interface is compromised.
General rule: When in doubt, strip and restart. Shipping a part with hidden adhesion failure is far more expensive than re-plating.
Process Context
The reverse etch dissolves the passive oxide layer while the substrate is immersed in chromic acid electrolyte. The instant switch to cathodic prevents re-passivation, allowing chromium to nucleate directly on the activated base metal. Any dwell at zero current allows the oxide to re-form, destroying adhesion. This is why the “no-dwell crossover” is the single most critical moment in the entire hard chrome process.
⚠ Safety — Current Ramp-Up & Etch-Strike
Active rectifier operations near chromic acid. Full Cr(VI) PPE mandatory. Reverse-etch increases mist generation — verify ventilation at full capacity. Never switch polarity on wet floor — use dry insulated matting. Verify polarity before energizing — wrong polarity dissolves the workpiece. Never touch bus bars while energized. If power fails during plating, DO NOT restart without consulting process engineer.

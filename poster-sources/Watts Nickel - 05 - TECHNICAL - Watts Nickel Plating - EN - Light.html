Cathode Efficiency
The Watts bath is over a century old and the most-used nickel electrolyte in the world. High cathode efficiency means most of your current deposits metal, not gas. But the remaining hydrogen co-evolution still loads enough atomic hydrogen to embrittle high-strength steel — the deposit is ductile, but the substrate underneath still needs the bake.

For plain Watts the axis is a sound deposit, not brightness — matte to semi-lustrous across the window is correct. Burning, roughness, and powdery edges mark the ceiling; thin, slow, hazy deposits mark the floor. Barrel work runs 5–15 ASF at 6–12 RPM — lower CD, longer cycles.

Anode Selection S-nickel vs non-sulfur

Sulfur-Depolarized (S-Rounds)

A trace of sulfur keeps the anode dissolving cleanly and efficiently at higher current densities. Best general-purpose choice — low anode polarization.

Rolled / Electrolytic (Non-Sulfur)

Dissolves less readily and can passivate if anode area or chloride is marginal — but introduces no sulfur into the system. Preferred where the end use is sulfur-sensitive (electroforming for thermal service, brazing preforms, diffusion applications). If running non-sulfur anodes, keep chloride toward the high end (50–60 g/L) and maintain generous anode area to prevent passivation.

Sulfur-free, defined: anode-borne trace sulfur does not make the deposit a sulfur-co-deposited layer the way a bright-nickel Class I carrier does. “Sulfur-free deposit” refers to the absence of organic sulfur-bearing brighteners. Where the spec demands a truly sulfur-free system end-to-end, use non-sulfur anodes and no sulfur-bearing additives.

⚠ Safety
Hot acidic nickel solution (pH 3.5–4.5, up to 150°F) — causes burns and is a dermal sensitizer. Soluble nickel salts are skin sensitizers and nickel compounds are classified as suspected carcinogens. Chronic nickel exposure causes allergic contact dermatitis (“nickel itch”) — cumulative and permanent once sensitized; minimize skin contact at all times. Air agitation generates nickel-containing mist — local exhaust ventilation mandatory; mist eliminators recommended. OSHA PEL for soluble nickel compounds: 1 mg/m³ TWA (as Ni). Rectifier = high amperage — LOTO for all maintenance. Use PP, CPVC, or lined tanks — nickel sulfate corrodes mild steel, and plain PVC is marginal above 140°F. Boric acid: a reproductive-hazard concern — avoid dust inhalation when charging or making additions, wear gloves, wash after handling. PPE: goggles + face shield, elbow-length nitrile chemical gloves, apron, boots, P100 or supplied-air respirator if ventilation is inadequate.

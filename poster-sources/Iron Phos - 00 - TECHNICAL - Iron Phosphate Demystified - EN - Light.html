Coating Weight (mg/ft²) — Amorphous Paint-Prep Film
Iron phosphate produces a thin amorphous film of vivianite-type iron phosphate (Fe₃(PO₄)₂) on steel. Typical coating weights of 25–100 mg/ft² provide excellent paint adhesion at a fraction of the cost and complexity of zinc phosphate. No grain refiner needed — the coating forms by direct chemical reaction with the iron substrate.

Why Iron Phosphate Dominates Spray Washer Lines
Iron phosphate is the workhorse of industrial spray-wash pre-treatment. It operates at lower temperatures, produces minimal sludge (no zinc or manganese to precipitate), and many formulations combine cleaning and phosphating into a single stage. The blue-to-purple iridescent film is thinner than zinc phosphate but provides excellent paint adhesion for powder coat, e-coat, and liquid spray. Lower operating cost per square foot makes it ideal for high-volume appliance, HVAC, and general industrial finishing.

Did You Know?
Iron phosphate is technically not a “coating” in the traditional sense — it is an amorphous conversion of the steel surface itself. Unlike crystalline zinc phosphate, iron phosphate produces no visible crystal structure under a microscope. Its coating weight is just 20–90 mg/ft², roughly 10× lighter than zinc phosphate. Despite being the simplest phosphate process, iron phosphate pretreatment dominates high-volume manufacturing: it runs in spray washers at line speeds exceeding 30 feet per minute, processing everything from HVAC ductwork to appliance panels. The process was first commercialized in the 1940s when manufacturers needed a faster, cheaper alternative to immersion zinc phosphate for painted sheet metal.

Iron Phosphate vs Zinc Phosphate when to use each

Iron Phosphate Choose When

  • Interior or mild-environment parts
  • Powder coat or e-coat topcoat over steel
  • High-volume spray washer lines
  • Lower operating cost is priority
  • Minimal sludge generation required

Zinc Phosphate Choose Instead

  • Exterior automotive body panels
  • Severe corrosion environments
  • Cold forming / wire drawing lubrication
  • Multi-metal substrates (steel + galvanized)
  • Maximum salt-spray hours required

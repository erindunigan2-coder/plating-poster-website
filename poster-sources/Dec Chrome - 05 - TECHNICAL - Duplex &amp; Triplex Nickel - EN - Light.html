The Corrosion Engine — Semi-Bright / Bright / µPorous Nickel Stack

The nickel system is the heart of decorative chrome. It provides all the corrosion protection. The chrome on top is purely cosmetic. Getting the layer ratios, sulfur content, and potential differences right is what separates a 32-hour CASS part from a warranty claim.

100–200 mV STEP Test — The Potential That Drives Lateral Corrosion
In a duplex system, bright nickel (containing sulfur) is anodic to semi-bright nickel (sulfur-free). This 100–200 mV potential difference forces corrosion to spread laterally through the bright layer instead of penetrating to the substrate. Semi-bright must be ≥60% of total nickel thickness. Microporous nickel adds >10,000 distributed corrosion cells/cm² in the chrome layer.

Semi-Bright Ni Must Be ≥60% of Total Nickel Thickness
The duplex system works because bright Ni (anodic, with sulfur) corrodes sacrificially to protect semi-bright Ni (cathodic, sulfur-free). If semi-bright is too thin, the corrosion path reaches the substrate before lateral spread dissipates the attack. ASTM B456 SC4 requires duplex or triplex nickel with semi-bright ≥60% of total Ni. The STEP test (ASTM B764) measures the potential difference between layers — 100–200 mV is the target range.
⚠ Safety Summary — Nickel Plating
Nickel compounds — skin sensitizer GHS Cat. 1; soluble nickel salts (NiSO₄, NiCl₂) are IARC Group 1 / GHS Cat. 1A carcinogen via inhalation. OSHA PEL 1 mg/m³ (soluble Ni). Boric acid — reproductive toxicant (GHS Cat. 1B). Brighteners — saccharin and organic additives — handle per SDS. Mist suppression and LEV at all nickel tanks. Nitrile gloves (not latex) for Ni contact. Emergency shower/eyewash within 10 sec. SDSs posted.

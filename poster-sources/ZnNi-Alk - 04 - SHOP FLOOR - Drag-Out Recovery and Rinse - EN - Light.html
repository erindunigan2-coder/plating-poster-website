<50
µS/cm — Final Rinse Conductivity Before Passivation
Above 50 µS/cm means alkaline chemistry is still on the parts. This will shift passivation pH, cause blotchy coatings, and waste expensive Cr3+ solution. Hit this number every time.
Operator Procedure — 6 Steps
1
Drip 10–15 s over plating tank. Angle rack 10–15° and let gravity pull solution back.
2
Dip in drag-out tank 20–30 s. This is a still tank — no overflow, no agitation.
3
Move through counter-current rinse (2 stages). Water flows opposite to part movement — clean water enters final stage only.
4
Check final rinse conductivity <50 µS/cm and pH 6.0–8.0 before moving to passivation. No exceptions.
5
Pump drag-out back to plating bath when Zn >2 g/L. Minimum once per week to prevent bacterial growth.
6
If pH >9 in final rinse — increase DI flow rate or notify supervisor. Alkaline carry-over is contaminating downstream.

⚠ Safety — Alkaline Drag-Out
Drag-out solution is alkaline pH 12–14 with dissolved nickel compounds. Skin contact causes chemical burns; eye contact is an emergency. Rinse water requires treatment before discharge — dissolved metals exceed POTW limits. Floors around rinse tanks are wet and slippery — anti-slip footwear, splash goggles, and chemical gloves required at all times.
Operator reference only. Follow your facility SOPs. Report any process deviations to your supervisor.

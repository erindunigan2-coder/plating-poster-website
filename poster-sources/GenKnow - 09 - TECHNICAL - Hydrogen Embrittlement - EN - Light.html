Causes, Risk & Relief — How Absorbed Hydrogen Causes Delayed Brittle Fracture in High-Strength Steel

The invisible threat: atomic hydrogen absorbed during plating, pickling, or cleaning diffuses to stress concentrators and grain boundaries, initiating cracks hours or days after processing — with no visible warning.

Bake Within 4 Hours — No Exceptions
Parts above 40 HRC (approx. 1,200 MPa / 175 ksi tensile strength) are at risk of hydrogen embrittlement. Per ASTM F519, AMS 2759/9, and aerospace specifications (BAC 5709), hydrogen-relief baking must begin within 4 hours of plating completion. Exceeding this window allows hydrogen to migrate irreversibly to trap sites, where it cannot be removed by baking.

H-E Risk by Process Hydrogen generation mechanisms

Critical H-E Knowledge Risk • Relief • Verification

⚠
Parts at Risk

High-strength steel ≥ 40 HRC (SAE 4340, 300M, maraging). Spring steel (music wire, Inconel X-750). Case-hardened & nitrided surfaces. Any fastener ≥ Class 10.9 / Grade 8. Precipitation-hardened stainless (17-4 PH, 15-5 PH).

🌡
Baking Parameters

190–220°C (375–425°F) for 4–24 hours per AMS 2759/9. Begin within 4 hours of plating. Duration scales with section thickness and hardness. ≥ 50 HRC requires minimum 23 hours. Cadmium-plated parts: 190°C max per AMS 2759/9 to avoid Cd deposit degradation.

🔬
Testing Methods

ASTM F519 sustained-load test: notched specimens under 75% NFS for 200 hours. Incremental step-loading test (ISL): rising load until failure. Slow strain-rate test (SSRT) per ASTM G129. Threshold stress intensity (KTH) measurement for critical applications.

H-E Prevention Do

  • Bake within 4 hours of plating — start the clock at tank exit, not end of shift
  • Use anodic electrocleaning for high-strength parts (H₂ evolves at counter electrode, not part)
  • Specify mechanical cleaning (grit blast, vapor hone) instead of acid pickle when possible
  • Log oven temperature with calibrated chart recorder or data logger per AMS 2750
  • Verify hardness of incoming parts — reject any > 40 HRC without H-E bake plan

Common Failures Avoid

  • Delaying bake past the 4-hour window — hydrogen becomes trapped irreversibly
  • Baking at too-low temperature (< 190°C) — insufficient diffusion rate to drive H out
  • Assuming zinc or cadmium plating is “low risk” — both generate significant atomic H
  • Skipping bake on “non-critical” hardened parts — any part ≥ 40 HRC is susceptible
  • Using cathodic electrocleaning on spring steel or landing gear components
Why “Delayed” Failure — The Mechanism Behind the Time Lag
Hydrogen embrittlement failures are uniquely dangerous because they are delayed. Atomic hydrogen absorbed during processing is initially dispersed throughout the lattice. Over hours to days, it migrates along concentration gradients toward stress concentrators — notch roots, thread roots, inclusions, and prior-austenite grain boundaries. As local H concentration exceeds a critical threshold, cohesive strength drops below the applied stress, and intergranular cracks initiate without plastic deformation. Parts can pass all initial inspections, meet dimensional and cosmetic requirements, and then fracture catastrophically under sustained static load in service — sometimes weeks after plating.

H-E Related Failures Field & service failures

Bolt Snap Under Static Load

High-strength fastener (≥ Grade 10.9) fractures at thread root under sustained torque. Intergranular fracture surface. Cause: plated without bake, or bake delayed beyond 4 hours. Per IFI 145 / ASTM F1941.

Landing Gear Cracks

Cadmium-plated 4340 steel landing gear develops cracks after return to service. Delayed fracture at stress riser. Traced to acid pickling before plating without adequate bake per BAC 5709.

Spring Fracture in Assembly

Music wire or Inconel springs break during assembly or early service. Brittle failure at coil ID (maximum stress). Zinc plating without post-plate bake. ASTM F519 test was not specified on PO.

Bracket Failure Weeks After Plating

Case-hardened bracket passes QC, ships to customer, fails 2–6 weeks later under constant load. Classic delayed H-E: hydrogen migrated to stress point over time. Bake was performed but at 150°C — below effective temperature.

⚠ Safety — Baking Oven Operations & High-Strength Part Handling
Burn hazard: Oven temperatures of 190–220°C cause severe contact burns — use heat-resistant gloves (rated ≥ 250°C), face shield, and tongs for loading/unloading. Fire risk: Parts contaminated with oil or solvent can ignite in the oven — ensure parts are clean and dry before baking. Ventilation: Cadmium-plated parts release toxic fumes if overheated — oven must have exhaust ventilation per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1027. Part handling: Do not drop or impact high-strength parts after plating and before bake — impact can trigger H-E fracture in charged parts.

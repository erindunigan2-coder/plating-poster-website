This Step May Be Prohibited by Customer Specification
Many rectifier busbar specifications explicitly prohibit anti-tarnish or passivation coatings on silver contact surfaces. Even nanometer-scale films increase contact resistance at high current. Do NOT apply anti-tarnish unless the customer specification allows it or requires it. When in doubt, skip this step and proceed directly to the final rinse and dry.

The Contact Resistance Trade-Off
Anti-tarnish extends the shelf life and handling window for silver-plated busbars significantly — from days to weeks or months before visible tarnish appears. However, the molecular film deposited by the anti-tarnish adds measurable contact resistance. For decorative or low-current applications, this is negligible. For rectifier busbars carrying hundreds or thousands of amps, even a few micro-ohms of additional contact resistance translates to real watts of waste heat at every bolted joint. This is why many high-current busbar specifications explicitly prohibit post-plate coatings. The alternative is to package immediately in sulfur-free sealed PE bags and minimize storage time.

Troubleshooting Anti-tarnish issues

Contact Resistance Too High

Anti-tarnish film too thick (over-treated) or wrong product for the application. Reduce immersion time. Verify product compatibility with customer spec. May need to strip and re-plate without anti-tarnish.

Uneven or Blotchy Appearance

Poor rinse before this step carried contaminants into anti-tarnish bath. Improve rinse quality. Check anti-tarnish bath for contamination. Ensure uniform agitation during immersion.

Silver Still Tarnishes Quickly

Anti-tarnish bath exhausted or too dilute. Check concentration per supplier TDS. Replenish or replace bath. Also verify downstream packaging is sulfur-free.

Customer Rejects Anti-Tarnish

Customer spec prohibits coatings but parts were treated anyway. Process error — parts may need to be stripped and re-plated. Verify spec requirements BEFORE processing any lot.

⚠ Safety
Hazard depends entirely on the actual product — confirm the chemistry against the SDS before handling. Benzotriazole / chromate-free organic inhibitors are relatively mild and cause skin and eye irritation with prolonged contact. However, many silver anti-tarnish baths are chromate-based (hexavalent chromium, Cr(VI)), a confirmed human carcinogen regulated under the OSHA Cr(VI) standard, REACH, and RoHS. If the bath is chromate/Cr(VI), treat it as a carcinogen: avoid all skin and eye contact and inhalation of mist, and follow the SDS exposure controls. Standard PPE required: chemical-resistant gloves, safety glasses, and apron. Ensure adequate ventilation; if the product is heated or generates mist, use local exhaust. Follow the supplier’s SDS for specific handling requirements.

Alkaline — Not Caustic. Aluminum-Safe Only.
Standard steel-line cleaners run pH 12–14 with free NaOH. They will etch and pit aluminum in under 60 seconds. Use a silicated or inhibited formulation designed for aluminum and its alloys.

Use This Correct

  • Silicated alkaline cleaner (low-NaOH, inhibited)
  • Products labeled “aluminum-safe” or “non-etch”
  • pH 9.5–11.0 — below the etch threshold for 6xxx alloys
  • Mild air agitation to lift soils without excessive foam

Not This Wrong

  • Standard steel-line alkaline cleaner (high free NaOH, pH 12+)
  • Caustic soak intended for ferrous metals
  • Solvent degreaser alone (leaves residue, no alkaline action)
  • Ultrasonic with wrong chemistry (can accelerate etch damage)
Process Insight
High-caustic formulations dissolve aluminum’s protective oxide and attack the base metal, creating a spongy, pitted surface. This damage propagates through every subsequent layer. A spongy substrate traps zincate solution, causes hydrogen pockets under the copper, and produces blistering that may not appear until the part is in service under thermal cycling. The cleaner is the cheapest step on the line — getting it wrong is the most expensive mistake.

Troubleshooting Cleaning failures

Water Break Test Fails

Parts not clean. Increase time or temperature. Check cleaner concentration — may be depleted. Look for silicone-based marking inks (hard to remove with standard alkaline).

Etching / Pitting Visible

Cleaner is too aggressive for aluminum. Check pH — if above 11.5, switch to an aluminum-safe product immediately. Check for caustic drag-in from adjacent steel line.

Foaming in Tank

Excess oil loading or wrong agitation. Skim surface oils. Reduce air agitation rate. Consider adding defoamer per supplier recommendation.

Parts Discolor After Clean

Cleaner attacking substrate — too hot, too long, or wrong product. Reduce temp to low end of range (54°C). Shorten immersion. Verify product is rated for 6xxx Al.

⚠ Safety
Alkaline cleaners cause chemical burns on skin and eyes. Wear face shield, chemical-resistant gloves, apron, and rubber boots. Tank temperature (54–66°C / 130–150°F) is hot enough to scald. If cleaner contacts skin, flush with water for 15 minutes. If it contacts eyes, flush immediately and seek medical attention.

Conversion Coating — Sealer
SEALER / TOPCOAT SEALER
Lock in corrosion protection with a final sealer coat
A sealer is the last chemical step before drying — it closes pores in phosphate, chromate, or anodize coatings and adds a supplemental barrier against corrosion. Skipping or rushing the sealer throws away the protection built in every prior step.

THE KEY NUMBER
100%
Every part must receive full sealer coverage with no drips, dry spots, or pooling. Verify concentration and temperature before each run
WHAT YOU MUST DO
1
Verify sealer concentration with a refractometer or titration — diluted sealer gives no protection
2
Confirm bath temperature is within spec (typically 140–180 °F / 60–82 °C for reactive sealers, ambient for wax/oil)
3
Immerse parts for the full specified dwell time — pulling early leaves pores open
4
Ensure parts enter the sealer clean and still wet from the prior rinse — dried-on salts block sealer penetration
5
Drain parts at an angle that prevents pooling in recesses — pooled sealer dries thick and flakes
COMMON PROBLEMS
Problem What to Look For What to Do
White residue or blushWhite powdery haze on the surface after drying, especially on flat areasSealer concentration is too high or dwell time too long — reduce concentration per supplier TDS and verify rinse quality before sealer
Corrosion in salt spray testRed or white rust appearing before the required hours in neutral salt spray (ASTM B117)Check sealer concentration, bath temperature, and dwell time — also verify the base coating (phosphate, chromate) is within spec before blaming the sealer
Sealer pooling or drippingThick buildup in holes, channels, or at the bottom edge of partsAdjust drain angle and drain time; reduce sealer viscosity if allowed; use air blow-off on recesses before oven entry
⚠ SAFETY
●Wear chemical splash goggles and nitrile gloves — sealers contain chromates, silicates, or organic solvents depending on type
●Reactive chromate sealers may contain hexavalent chromium — verify SDS and follow your facility's Cr⁶⁺ exposure control plan
●Oil-based and wax sealers are flammable — no open flames or hot surfaces near the sealer station
●If sealer contacts eyes, flush with water for 15 minutes and report to your supervisor immediately
Consult the supervisor before adjusting process parameters.

RINSE
POST-ANODIZE

Remove Acid Before Dye or Seal — Protect the Pores

Acid carry-over kills dye color and prevents sealing. Rinse thoroughly.

µS/cm — Tightest Rinse on the Line
Residual sulfuric acid in the pores will inhibit dye absorption and interfere with sealing. DI water is strongly recommended for the final stage. Use a double or triple counter-current cascade for best results. Measure conductivity with a handheld meter — do not rely on visual clarity alone.

Procedures 5 steps

1
Transfer from anodize immediately. Do not let oxide dry — dried acid causes staining.
2
Immerse fully and agitate vigorously — 10+ vertical strokes. Acid hides in blind holes.
3
Double or triple rinse. Verify conductivity <100 µS/cm in the final stage.
4
Check conductivity with a handheld meter. Log the reading — if >100 µS/cm, repeat the rinse cycle.
5
Transfer to dye or seal immediately. Open pores begin to close in air — maximum 5 minutes between rinse and next tank.

Do best

  • Use DI water in final rinse stage
  • Run counter-current cascade
  • Transfer to dye within 5 min of rinse
  • Monitor conductivity every 2 hours
  • Log every rinse reading in the shift book

Don’t avoid

  • Use warm/hot water (premature pore closure)
  • Let parts sit in air after rinsing
  • Skip DI on the final stage for dye work
  • Ignore rising conductivity readings
  • Drag parts across tank edges — scratches the oxide layer
⚠ Safety — Residual Acid
Rinse water carries residual H₂SO₄. First-stage rinse can be pH 2–3 — enough for skin irritation and eye damage. Wear chemical-resistant gloves and splash goggles when handling parts or checking tanks. Floors are wet and slippery — anti-slip footwear required at all times in the rinse area. Keep the eyewash station accessible and tested weekly. Report any spills or splashes to your supervisor immediately.

Operator reference only. Verify all parameters against your chemistry supplier’s TDS and applicable specifications before production use.

Process Stages — Clean → Rinse → Activate → Rinse → Plate → Rinse → Post-Treat → Rinse / Dry
Semi-bright nickel is a Watts-type bath with a sulfur-free leveler — not a brightener, and not nothing at all. The deposit is satin, leveled, ductile, and sulfur-free: the base layer of the duplex decorative stack, deliberately more noble than the bright nickel plated over it. Every upstream stage either protects the bath or poisons it. There is no neutral step.
Critical Control Points WATCH THESE 3
01 — Cleaning
The leveler fills scratches, not dirt — a prep miss plates through every layer above it, bright nickel and chrome included. Water-break clean or stop the rack.
05 — Plating
Keep it sulfur-free. Leveler dosed on amp-hours, not by eye. Burning = pH too high or temperature too low — not “add more amps.”
06 — Firewall Rinse
The only barrier between the two nickel chemistries. Sulfur-free leveler must not ride forward into the bright bath — bright additive must never wash back.

Semi-Bright · Satin / Leveled (this series)
Bright Nickel · Full Mirror Bright
  • It is NOT mirror-bright — the finish is satin / semi-lustrous, by design. Full brightness comes from the bright nickel layer plated over it.
  • It is sulfur-FREE (<0.005% S) — no sulfur-bearing Class I carrier, no Class II brightener. This is what makes it more noble than the bright layer.
  • It is NOT the top coat — it is the bottom layer of a duplex stack: semi-bright → bright → chrome.
  • It is deliberately MORE NOBLE than the bright nickel above it — so the bright layer corrodes sacrificially and protects it. This is measured, not assumed (ASTM B764 STEP test).
⚠  Do NOT dose bright-nickel (sulfur-bearing) brightener into a semi-bright tank. A single drag-over of bright-bath additive can add sulfur to the semi-bright deposit, collapse the STEP potential difference, and kill the corrosion performance of the whole stack.
Spec tip — the decorative stack (semi-bright + bright + chrome) is governed by ASTM B456 and verified by the ASTM B764 STEP test (use current revisions). Bake high-strength steel per ASTM B850.
⚠ Line Safety
Hot alkaline cleaners, strong mineral acids, and hot acidic nickel solution — nickel salts are skin sensitizers and nickel compounds are classified as suspected carcinogens. OSHA PEL, soluble nickel: 1 mg/m³ TWA (as Ni). Nickel itch is cumulative and permanent once sensitized — gloves whenever handling parts, racks, or wet work. Rectifiers = high amperage: LOTO before reaching into any tank. Each stage poster carries its own full safety panel.

Key Rules activation

1Dip time is seconds, not minutes — over-activation etches the substrate and ruins adhesion
2Never let parts dry between activation and plating — oxide reforms immediately
3Replace acid when Fe builds up — spent acid re-deposits smut instead of removing it
4Rinse before and after acid dip — alkaline drag-in neutralizes the acid; acid drag-out contaminates the copper bath
5If parts have been idle or dried out, re-activate — passive oxide reforms within minutes in air
Why Over-Activation is Worse Than Under-Activation
Under-activation leaves oxide, which causes peeling later. But over-activation dissolves base metal, roughens the surface, and makes copper leveling nearly impossible. A few extra seconds in strong HCl can create pits that telegraph through every layer up to the chrome finish.
⚠ Safety
HCl gives off strong fumes at room temperature and causes burns, so local exhaust is required. H₂SO₄ reacts exothermically with water and causes severe burns, so always add acid to water, never water to acid. Splash goggles, a face shield, and chemical-resistant gloves are mandatory, and an emergency shower and eyewash must be within 10 seconds of the station.

Do not skip any stage. Each step prepares the surface for the next one. The autocatalytic reaction deposits nickel-phosphorus alloy uniformly on any catalytic surface — but only if that surface is properly prepared.

Autocatalytic Deposition
Hypophosphite (NaH₂PO₂) reduces Ni²⁺ ions directly on the catalytic surface. Each fresh nickel-phosphorus layer catalyzes the next cycle. The reaction is self-sustaining as long as pH, temperature, and nickel concentration stay in spec. No external current is needed — the deposit builds uniformly regardless of part geometry.

Metal Turnovers & Bath Life
Each cycle of Ni²⁺ replenishment produces orthophosphite (HPO₃²−) as a byproduct that cannot be removed by treatment. When orthophosphite exceeds ~120 g/L (typically 4–6 MTO), deposit quality degrades — pitting increases, stress rises, and rate drops. The bath must be partially or fully dumped. Track MTO by dividing total nickel replenished by original nickel charge. Fresh baths plate fastest; plan critical work early in the bath cycle.

⚠ Safety Summary — All 7 Stages
This line handles acids, caustics, and hot solutions at 85–91°C. Wear splash goggles, chemical-resistant gloves, and apron at all times. EN bath decomposes if stabilizer is depleted or temperature spikes — releases hydrogen and potentially phosphine gas (PH₃, TLV 0.05 ppm). If bath shows sudden turbidity or temperature climbing on its own: STOP, leave the area, call supervisor.

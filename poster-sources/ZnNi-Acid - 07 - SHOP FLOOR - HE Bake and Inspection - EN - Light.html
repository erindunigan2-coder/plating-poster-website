Bake Procedure
1
Check if bake is required: UTS >1,050 MPa (>39 HRC) = YES. Check the drawing or spec sheet.
2
Start bake within 4 hours of plating (1–2 h preferred). Delay increases HE risk exponentially.
3
Load parts — do NOT stack. Every surface must be exposed to oven air for uniform H₂ diffusion.
4
Thermocouple ON THE PART. Time starts when the part hits 191°C, not the oven setpoint.
5
Bake: 39–48 HRC = 14 h min. >48 HRC = 23 h min + ASTM F519 sustained-load test.
6
Cool to <50°C before handling. Do NOT quench — thermal shock can crack high-strength steel.
Final Inspection Checklist
  • Thickness: XRF at 3–5 locations per part — 8–15 µm per spec
  • Alloy: XRF alloy mode — 12–15% Ni required
  • Adhesion: ASTM B571 bend or thermal shock — no flaking, peeling, or blistering
  • Appearance: 100% visual — bright/semi-bright (normal for acid); uniform, no stains, burns, pits
  • Salt Spray: ASTM B117 panels per lot — 500–1,500+ h typical for acid
  • Friction (Fasteners): Torque-tension test — K = 0.12–0.18
Troubleshooting
  • Coating peels in bend test: Contamination before plating, poor activation, or excess thickness. Review pretreatment.
  • Parts crack days/weeks later: Hydrogen embrittlement — bake was late, too short, or too cool. Audit bake log and thermocouple placement.
  • Ni% out of spec: Alloy drifted during plating. Check bath chemistry, current density, and temperature. Ni <12% = poor corrosion.
  • Salt spray failure: Thin deposit, low Ni%, poor passivation, or missing sealer. Check: thickness → alloy → passivation → sealer.
⚠ Safety
Oven at 191–220°C — severe burn risk. Heat-resistant gloves and face shield required when loading/unloading. Cool-down staging area required. Parts remain dangerously hot for 30+ minutes after removal. XRF analyzers use X-ray radiation. Follow manufacturer safety protocols. Never point at people. H₂ off-gassing during bake. Ensure oven ventilation — hydrogen is flammable at 4–75% in air.

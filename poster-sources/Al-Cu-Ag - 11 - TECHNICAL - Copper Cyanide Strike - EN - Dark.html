Thin Adherent Copper Seed Layer — Hot Entry Required

Deposit a thin (1–3 µm) highly adherent copper seed layer over the zincate coating. The cyanide complex prevents immersion displacement that would destroy adhesion. Current must be ON before parts enter the bath.

HOT ENTRY
Current ON Before Parts Enter Bath
Apply current to the rack before immersing parts. If parts sit in the cyanide strike without current, the zincate layer dissolves and adhesion is permanently destroyed. Free cyanide must remain high relative to copper metal to suppress immersion deposition. This is a non-negotiable process requirement.

Why Hot Entry Is Mandatory
Without applied current, copper ions in solution will spontaneously displace the zinc layer through an uncontrolled immersion reaction. This destroys the carefully built zincate adhesion layer. By applying current before immersion, electrodeposition dominates and copper deposits uniformly over the intact zincate, creating a strongly adherent seed layer for the full copper plate that follows.

Anode Selection

Steel (inert) anodes are preferred in the strike bath. The strike's low-copper/high-free-cyanide condition tends to passivate copper anodes, and inert anodes help keep the copper content low — exactly what a strike requires. Copper is then replenished only by CuCN additions, allowing precise control of the Cu:CN ratio.

Monitor copper metal concentration regularly. Replenish CuCN as copper depletes through plating.

Carbonate Control

Sodium carbonate accumulates from CO₂ absorption and cyanide oxidation at the anode. Keep below 15 g/L. High carbonate reduces conductivity, narrows the operating window, and can cause rough deposits. Remove by cooling crystallization if levels exceed limits.

Na₂CO₃ <15 g/L — test weekly in production environments.

Troubleshooting Copper strike issues

Poor Adhesion / Blistering

Parts entered bath without current (cold entry). Zincate dissolved before copper deposited. Verify hot entry procedure. Also check free cyanide — if too low, immersion deposition can occur even with current.

Dark or Burned Deposit

Current density too high or free cyanide too low. Reduce current. Check NaCN levels — free cyanide should be 11–15 g/L. Also verify temperature is within range.

No Deposit / Thin Coverage

Copper concentration depleted. Test CuCN level and replenish. Also check electrical connections — poor contact on the rack will prevent current flow to affected parts.

Rough Deposit

Carbonate too high (>15 g/L) or particulate contamination. Check Na₂CO₃ level. Carbon-treat if organic contamination suspected. Filter solution continuously.

⚠ Safety
CYANIDE BATH — the cyanide salts in this tank (NaCN and CuCN) are lethal by ingestion, inhalation, and skin absorption. This is the first cyanide bath in the process line. NEVER mix cyanide solutions with acid — this generates deadly hydrogen cyanide (HCN) gas. Maintain bath pH >10 at all times. Operate under slot ventilation at minimum 100 fpm face velocity. HCN monitors must be installed and calibrated. Cyanide antidote kit must be accessible within 30 seconds. Wear chemical-resistant gloves, face shield, and apron. Know the location of the emergency shower and eyewash station.

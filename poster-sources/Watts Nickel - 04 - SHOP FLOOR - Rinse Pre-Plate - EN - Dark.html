Conductivity Target — The Bath’s Most Important Barrier
The Watts bath operates hot and is maintained rather than replaced — a well-run bath runs for years. Every milligram of acid, dissolved iron, copper, or zinc that enters through poor rinsing accumulates permanently. This is the single most important contamination barrier on the line.

Rinse pH below 4 = excessive acid drag-in. Municipal water adds calcium and chloride — DI strongly preferred this close to the bath. A double counter-current rinse is the standard answer when the number won’t hold.
Iron does not leave — above ~25 ppm Fe in the bath: haze, roughness, raised stress, lost ductility. This rinse is the primary defense — keep the pickle fresh and rinse aggressively.

✓ Do
  • Run double counter-current rinsing — standard on nickel lines
  • Hold 15–20 sec drain before and after the rinse
  • Move parts to the nickel tank within 30 seconds
✗ Don’t
  • Feed this rinse from a spent, iron-loaded pickle
  • Let parts sit and dry — oxide re-forms and adhesion dies
  • Accept > 200 µS/cm “just this rack” — the bath remembers
⚠ Safety
Dilute acid residue in the rinse water. Goggles, chemical gloves, non-slip footwear. Parts must move quickly to the nickel tank — plan the transfer path before lifting the rack; no delays, no detours.

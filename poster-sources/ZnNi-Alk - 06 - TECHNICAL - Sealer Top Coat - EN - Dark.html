Coefficient of Friction — Torque-Tension for Fasteners
Automotive OEMs (Ford, GM, VW) specify K = 0.12–0.18 for threaded fasteners. The sealer controls this number. Out-of-spec friction means joints are either over-torqued (bolt failure) or under-torqued (loose joints). Test every rack. The sealer fills micro-cracks in the passivation film and adds an extra barrier layer. This step often doubles the salt spray hours compared to passivation alone.

Key Procedures 7 steps

1
Verify sealer concentration by refractometer — typically 5–15% v/v. Low concentration = thin film, poor friction control.
2
Verify temp 20–35°C (some silicate types require 40–50°C). Check supplier TDS.
3
Immerse passivated parts 30–60 s with gentle agitation. Ensure full coverage on all surfaces.
4
Withdraw slowly 2–5 cm/s for uniform coverage. Fast withdrawal = drip marks and pooling.
5
Dry at 80–120°C for 10–20 min. Silicate: 80°C/15 min. Silane: 100–120°C/10–15 min. Organic polymer: 80–100°C/10–20 min.
6
Test friction coefficient on 3–5 parts per rack. Record K-factor. Reject rack if out of 0.12–0.18 range.
7
Visual inspect for drip marks, pooling, bare spots. These indicate withdrawal speed or concentration issues.

Sealer Chemistry Comparison
Silicate-based: Inorganic, excellent corrosion resistance, lower friction values (K 0.10–0.14). Best for salt spray performance. Cure at 80°C.  •  Silane-based: Organic/inorganic hybrid, good adhesion to passivation layer, moderate friction control. Cure at 100–120°C. Flammable — requires LEL monitoring.  •  Organic polymer: Wax or PTFE-modified polymers, primary purpose is friction control. Adjustable K-factor by concentration. Cure at 80–100°C. Most forgiving process window.

Do best

  • Verify concentration every shift by refractometer
  • Test friction on 3–5 parts per rack
  • Control withdrawal speed for uniform film
  • Keep oven temperature <150°C to protect passivation
  • Inspect for complete coverage before sending to cure oven
  • Filter sealer tank continuously to remove particulates
  • Log K-factor results per rack for SPC tracking

Don’t avoid

  • Mix different sealer chemistries in the same tank
  • Apply sealer to wet/dripping parts
  • Skip sealer for automotive OEM work
  • Re-dip dried parts — double film causes peeling
  • Stack sealed parts before cure is complete — sealer transfers between surfaces
  • Use tap water for sealer make-up — hard water causes haze and poor adhesion

Troubleshooting

Friction Out of Spec

Concentration drift or uneven drying. Check refractometer reading; verify oven temp uniformity; adjust withdrawal speed.

Drip Marks / Pooling

Withdrawal too fast or parts not oriented for drainage. Slow withdrawal to 2–3 cm/s; orient blind holes downward.

Sealer Peeling / Flaking

Applied too thick (double dip or high concentration), or over-baked. Reduce concentration; check oven temp <150°C.

Cloudy / Hazy Appearance

Hard water in sealer or contamination. Check DI water quality; filter sealer tank; check for cross-contamination.

Poor Adhesion to Passivation

Inadequate rinse between passivation and sealer, or passivation not fully cured. Ensure rinse water is <5 µS/cm; allow passivation to stabilize before sealing.

Uneven Coating Thickness

Racking too dense or poor part orientation. Space parts for free drainage; ensure blind holes face downward; check for air pockets during immersion.

⚠ Safety — Sealers & Ovens
PPE: Chemical-resistant gloves (nitrile), splash goggles, and apron when handling sealers — most are mildly acidic to neutral but cause skin/eye irritation on contact. Flammability: Some silane-based sealers have flash points below 40°C — check SDS, store in approved flammable-liquid cabinets per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.106, ground containers during transfer. Oven hazards: Drying ovens at 80–120°C — wear heat-resistant gloves rated to 200°C; allow parts to cool before handling. Ventilation: Vent oven exhaust to outside; monitor LEL for silane-based products in enclosed ovens. Eyewash and safety shower within 10 seconds per ANSI Z358.1. Class B extinguisher near silane-based sealer storage.

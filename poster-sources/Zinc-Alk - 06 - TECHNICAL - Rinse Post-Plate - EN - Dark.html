Wash Off the Caustic Before the Passivate

Caustic into chromate is a guaranteed reject.

NaOH drag-out into the acidic passivate neutralizes the chromate, raises pH, and produces blotchy, thin passivation. This rinse controls passivate bath life and finish quality.

Key Procedures 7 steps

1
Transfer from plating bath. Drain 15–20 sec over plating tank first (drag-out recovery).
2
Immerse fully in first rinse stage. Agitate vigorously — 5–8 strokes.
3
Hold 30–60 sec.
4
Double rinse: transfer to second stage and repeat agitation.
5
Check conductivity. Target <200 µS/cm. Flag and correct if rising.
6
Visually inspect fresh zinc while in rinse: Last check before passivation.
7
Drain 15–20 sec before entering the passivate tank.
Visual Inspection Guide
✓ Accept
Bright, uniform silver. Smooth, even coverage.
✗ Dark Streaks
Iron contamination or anode issue in plating bath.
✗ Skip Plate
Bare substrate exposed. Poor cleaning or activation.
✗ Roughness
Particulate, high carbonates, or anode sludge in bath.
✗ White Haze
Hard water spots or zinc oxidation. Use DI water.
✗ Blistering / Peeling
H₂ entrapment or poor adhesion. Check activation step.

Do best practice

  • Inspect zinc in this rinse — last chance before passivate
  • Use double rinse to protect the passivate bath
  • Drain thoroughly — passivate dilution shortens bath life
  • Log conductivity and pH every shift

Don’t avoid

  • Skip inspection and discover defects after passivation
  • Rely on a single dip rinse for this critical step
  • Rush parts into passivate dripping water
  • Blame the passivate when the real problem is this rinse

Troubleshooting Common issues

Passivate Bath pH Climbing

NaOH drag-in through this rinse. Tighten rinse conductivity; add second rinse stage; extend drain time.

Blotchy Passivate Color

Residual caustic on parts entering passivate. Improve rinse agitation and time; check conductivity.

Passivate Bath Life Short

Excessive drag-in (water dilution + chemistry). Better drain time; use DI water; counter-current rinse.

White Haze on Zinc

Hard water spots or zinc oxidation from long transfer time. Use DI water; reduce transfer time to passivate.

⚠ Hydrogen Embrittlement Alert
For hardened steel parts (>40 HRC or >180 ksi tensile), the bake-out timer starts now. Parts must be in the oven within 4 hours of plating at 375±25°F for ≥8 hours per ASTM B850. Do not delay at this stage.
⚠ Safety
Dilute NaOH residue. Freshly plated zinc surfaces are chemically active — handle with clean gloves to avoid fingerprint contamination. PPE: chemical splash goggles, chemical-resistant gloves (clean), non-slip footwear.

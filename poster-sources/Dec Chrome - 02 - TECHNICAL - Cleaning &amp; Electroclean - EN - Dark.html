Soak Clean + Electrocleaning — The Foundation of Every Good Finish

If the part is not perfectly clean, nothing downstream will stick. Soak cleaners remove bulk soils; electrocleaning uses gas evolution to scrub the surface at the microscopic level. The water-break test is your go/no-go gate.

Allowable Water-Break Time — If Water Beads, the Part Is Not Clean
A clean surface holds a continuous water film for ≥30 seconds with zero break. Any bead or dry spot means residual oil, oxide, or surfactant remains. Parts that fail water-break will show blistering, skip-plate, or poor adhesion in nickel and chrome. Clean first, verify, then proceed.

Water-Break Test — The Go / No-Go Gate
After the final rinse following electrocleaning, flood the part surface with DI water. A perfectly clean surface holds a continuous, unbroken water film for ≥30 seconds. Any bead, dry spot, or film break means residual contamination. Parts failing water-break must return to cleaning — proceeding to activation with contamination guarantees adhesion failure in downstream deposits.

⚠ Safety Summary — Cleaning & Electroclean
Alkaline cleaners — pH 12–14; cause severe burns on contact; wear face shield, chemical-resistant gloves, and apron. Electroclean — electrical hazard at 4–8 V DC; ensure proper grounding and insulation. Hot solutions — 60–80°C soak tanks; thermal burn risk. Steam and mist inhalation hazard — local exhaust ventilation required. Emergency shower/eyewash within 10 sec. SDSs posted.

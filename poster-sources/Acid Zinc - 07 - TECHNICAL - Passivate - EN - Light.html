Acid Zinc Passivation Note
Acid zinc’s brighter, smoother as-plated deposit often produces more uniform passivation appearance. However, acid zinc deposits have slightly higher internal stress — use a passivate formulated for acid zinc, not one designed for alkaline zinc.
Addendum — Final Rinse & Dry
  • DI water rinse: Ambient temperature, gentle immersion, 15–30 sec
  • Warm air dry: 120–150°F max for trivalent (film cracks above 150°F)
  • Full cure: 24 hours minimum before packing or testing

Troubleshooting 5 common issues

Rainbow / Iridescent Film

Cause: pH too low; time too long
Fix: Check pH; reduce immersion time

Pale / Thin Film

Cause: pH too high; bath depleted
Fix: Lower pH; replenish per TDS

Blotchy / Uneven Color

Cause: Residual chloride; uneven zinc
Fix: Improve rinse; check plating uniformity

Film Rubs Off

Cause: pH out of range; insufficient cure
Fix: Check pH; allow 24-hr cure before handling

Passivate Bath Life Short

Cause: Chloride drag-in; zinc dissolution
Fix: Improve post-plate rinse quality

⚠ Trivalent Passivate Safety
Acidic (pH 1.5–3.5), causes chemical burns. PPE: goggles + face shield, chemical-resistant gloves, apron. Flush skin/eyes with water for 15 minutes if contact occurs.
☢ Hexavalent Passivate Safety
Known carcinogen. OSHA PEL 5 µg/m³. Supplied-air respirator, full protective suit, medical surveillance per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1026. Hexavalent chromium waste is RCRA hazardous — manage per facility permit.

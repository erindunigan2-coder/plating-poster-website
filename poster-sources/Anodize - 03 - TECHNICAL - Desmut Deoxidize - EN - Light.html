DESMUT
Remove Smut Before Anodize
Acid desmut dissolves the dark smut layer (Si, Cu, Fe, Mn residues) left by alkaline etching without attacking the aluminum substrate. Multiple chemistry options exist — nitric acid is the most common, but sulfuric/peroxide and proprietary blends are widely used depending on alloy, regulation, and shop preference. The choice of desmut chemistry is driven by three factors: alloy composition (high-Si castings need HF), environmental permits (NOx limits), and downstream requirements (chromic acid desmut for mil-spec work).

Key Procedures 5 steps

1
Transfer from rinse immediately. Do not allow parts to dry — dried smut is harder to remove.
2
Immerse fully. Agitate to ensure acid contacts all surfaces, especially blind holes and recesses.
3
Hold 1–3 minutes (up to 5 for heavy smut on cast alloys). Watch for smut to lift.
4
Inspect visually. Surface should be uniformly bright or matte with no dark patches.
5
Rinse thoroughly before entering anodize tank — acid drag-in contaminates the bath.

Many facilities use non-nitric desmuts due to NOx fume generation, wastewater nitrogen discharge limits, or local air quality regulations. Sulfuric/peroxide blends are the most common nitric-free alternative. Always match desmut chemistry to your alloy, specification requirements, and environmental permits.

Troubleshooting

Residual Smut After Desmut

Acid too weak or time too short. For high-Si alloys, switch to HNO₃/HF blend. Check dissolved Al level.

Pitting in Desmut

HF concentration too high or time too long. Reduce HF to 1%. Never use HF on thin-wall parts without testing.

Uneven Finish

Poor etch upstream or inconsistent alloy composition. Verify etch uniformity; check incoming material certs.

Rapid Acid Depletion

Caustic drag-in from poor rinsing at Step 02. Improve post-etch rinse before addressing desmut chemistry.

Brown/Orange Fumes (NOx)

Normal for nitric acid desmut. Ensure scrubber is operating; check face velocity at tank lip. If excessive, reduce acid concentration or switch to non-nitric chemistry.

Etching in Desmut Tank

HF concentration too high or wrong acid mix. Desmut should dissolve smut without attacking base metal. Reduce HF; verify acid concentration by titration.

Do best

  • Titrate acid concentration at start of shift
  • Track dissolved Al to know dump point
  • Rinse well after desmut — acid drag-in harms anodize
  • Test HF blends on coupons before production
  • Check desmut concentration by titration at the start of each shift

Don’t avoid

  • Use chromic acid desmut unless required by specification — strictly controlled Cr(VI) process
  • Skip desmut on cast alloys (smut causes defects)
  • Overextend HF immersion (pitting risk)
  • Let desmut sit idle without circulation
  • Skip desmut on high-copper alloys — smut causes severe color defects
⚠ Safety — Desmut Acids
Nitric acid (HNO₃) — strong oxidizer, severe burns, toxic NOx fumes (brown gas). Sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) — severe burns; mixing with H₂O₂ is exothermic — add peroxide to acid slowly, never reverse. H₂O₂ (35% industrial) — strong oxidizer, thermal burns, reacts violently with organics. Hydrofluoric acid (HF), if used, is life-threatening — penetrates skin painlessly and attacks bone calcium; requires immediate calcium gluconate treatment. PPE: splash goggles + face shield, chemical gloves (HF-rated neoprene if HF present), mandil, chemical boots. HF-specific first aid kit must be at station. Nitric acid fumes are highly corrosive to lung tissue. Ensure tank ventilation is operating before opening the tank or starting immersion. If HF is used, a calcium gluconate gel kit must be at the workstation — skin exposure to HF requires immediate treatment, even for small contact areas. Post HF exposure protocols at the tank.

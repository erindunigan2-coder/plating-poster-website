Process Stages
Clean → Activate → Copper → Nickel (multi-layer) → Chrome → Post-Treat. Each stage has specific chemistry, temp, and time.

Key Rules process flow

1Always rinse between stages — drag-out from one tank contaminates the next
2Semi-bright Ni must go before bright Ni — order creates the corrosion potential that protects parts
3Chrome is the thinnest layer (<1 µm) — the nickel system below does the real work
4Skip microporous Ni at your peril — without it, corrosion concentrates in deep pits
5Post-treatment must reduce residual hex chrome — environmental and quality requirement
⚠ Safety
Hexavalent chrome (Cr VI) is an IARC Group 1 carcinogen with an OSHA PEL of 5 µg/m³, so mist suppressants and local exhaust are mandatory. Nickel is a skin sensitizer, and its soluble forms are Category 1A carcinogens. Acid copper uses H₂SO₄, which causes burns. Cleaners run at pH 12–14 and also burn. Wear a full-face respirator at the chrome tank, and keep an emergency shower and eyewash within 10 seconds.

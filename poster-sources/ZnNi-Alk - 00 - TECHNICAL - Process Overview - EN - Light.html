Nickel in Deposit — The Critical Alloy Window
ASTM B841 and MIL-DTL-81706 require 12–15% Ni by weight for maximum corrosion resistance. Below 12% the deposit behaves like pure zinc; above 15% the alloy phase shifts and corrosion performance drops. Each stage must be controlled independently. Alkaline zinc-nickel demands tighter control than acid — temperature and additive balance are especially critical. The alkaline bath operates at pH 14 with no brightener system — deposit quality depends entirely on zinc-to-nickel metal ratio, caustic concentration, and additive balance. Hull cell testing every 4 hours is standard practice.

Why Zinc-Nickel?
4× corrosion life vs pure zinc — the gamma-phase alloy is inherently more noble.
Wear resistance — harder deposit survives assembly torque and vibration.
Salt spray — 1,000+ hours red rust with trivalent passivation and sealer.
RoHS compliant — Cr3+ passivation eliminates hexavalent chromium.
Automotive OEM standard — required by VW TL 233, GM GMW 3359, Ford WSS-M21P40.
⚠️
ELV Directive & RoHS restrict hexavalent chromium (Cr6+) in passivation. Trivalent Cr3+ is the industry standard for all new applications. Legacy Cr6+ lines are being phased out worldwide.
⚠ Line Safety Overview
NaOH (caustic soda) causes severe chemical burns — pH 14, immediate skin destruction. H&sub2;SO&sub4; and HCl fumes in acid activation — ventilate at >1 m/s face velocity. Nickel compounds are Category 1A carcinogens by inhalation — airborne Ni must stay <0.1 mg/m³. Hydrogen gas generated at cathode — explosive in air at 4–75% LEL; ventilate continuously. HE bake mandatory for high-strength steel (>40 HRC) within 4 hours of plating per ASTM F519. Report any chemical spills, unusual odors, or equipment malfunctions to your supervisor immediately. Never work alone on the plating line. Post emergency contact numbers and evacuation routes at both ends of the line. Verify fume extraction is operational before opening any tank lids during startup.

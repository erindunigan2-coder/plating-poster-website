CLEANING

Soak Clean + Electroclean — Two-Stage Surface Prep

A clean part is a plateable part — every skip-plate, blister, and adhesion failure traces back to a soil that survived this step. Acid Zn-Ni is less forgiving of residual soils than alkaline because the lower pH has no stripping action on organics.

Stage A: Soak Clean → Stage B: Electroclean
Two cleaning stages in series. Soak dissolves bulk oils and greases by heat and alkalinity. Electroclean scrubs residual films with gas evolution at the part surface. Both must pass water-break test before proceeding. Critical for acid Zn-Ni — unlike alkaline baths, acid chloride has zero stripping ability. If oil reaches the acid bath, it causes defects on every part until carbon-treated. Monitor both baths independently — the soak clean sees the heaviest soil load and degrades faster, while the electroclean must stay cleaner to protect the acid bath downstream.

Key Procedures 8 steps — Soak + Electroclean

1
Verify soak tank temp 60–75°C and alkalinity 60–80 g/L NaOH by titration.
2
Immerse 5–15 min; skim floating oil at start of shift.
3
Skim floating oil and check surfactant level — surfactant depletes as it emulsifies oil. Replenish per supplier TDS; low surfactant allows oil to re-deposit on parts after withdrawal.
4
Water-break test after soak; if pass, transfer to electroclean. If fail, extend time or replenish cleaner — acid Zn-Ni will not compensate for residual soils.
5
Electroclean: cathodic 30–60 s then anodic 15–30 s — MUST finish on anodic to strip smut.
6
Current density 5–10 A/dm²; voltage 4–8 V.
7
Water-break test after electroclean — must hold 30 s unbroken.
8
Transfer to acid activation within 60 s — do not allow parts to dry. Any organic film surviving this point will cause skip-plate in the acid bath.

Do best

  • Water-break test every rack
  • Skim oil every shift
  • Final cycle anodic always
  • Maintain rinse <200 µS/cm
  • Check cleaner concentration and temperature at start of shift
  • Log cleaner replenishment volume and date — track consumption vs. throughput

Don’t avoid

  • Let bath drop below 55°C
  • Skip alkalinity titration
  • Use cathodic-only on high-strength steel
  • Assume the acid bath will strip organics — it will not
  • Skip the water break test to save time — it catches 90% of cleaning failures
  • Drag excess soak clean into electroclean — drip time 10–15 s over soak tank

Troubleshooting

Water-Break Failure

Oil contamination or low alkalinity. Check NaOH concentration; skim surface; extend immersion time; add surfactant.

Uneven Gas Evolution

Poor electrical contact or passivated rack tips. Clean and re-tin contacts; verify rectifier connections.

Dark Smut After Electroclean

Finishing on cathodic cycle deposits metallic smut. Always end on anodic. If smut persists, acid dip will remove.

High Bath Contamination

Oil dragged into electroclean shortens bath life. Skim soak clean frequently; maintain oil removal system. Replace electroclean chemistry on a scheduled basis.

Skip-Plate in Acid Bath

Residual organics from inadequate cleaning — acid Zn-Ni has no stripping action on oils. Return to soak clean; verify water-break; consider stronger surfactant.

Blistering After Plating

Hydrogen embrittlement from excessive cathodic time in electroclean, or residual cleaning film trapped under deposit. Reduce cathodic cycle duration; verify final anodic polish; confirm water-break.

⚠ Safety — Caustic + Current + Heat
NaOH causes severe chemical burns — immediate tissue damage at working concentrations. H₂ gas generated at cathode — ensure ventilation >1 m/s face velocity over tank; explosive at 4–75% in air. Tanks operate at 65°C — thermal burn hazard on splash contact. Rectifier lockout/tagout required before any rack or anode maintenance. PPE: chemical splash goggles, face shield, nitrile/neoprene gloves, apron.

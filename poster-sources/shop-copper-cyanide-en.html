Electroplating — Copper Plate (Cyanide)
COPPER PLATE (CYANIDE)
Alkaline cyanide copper electrodeposition for strike and plating applications
Cyanide copper plating provides excellent adhesion on difficult substrates including zinc die castings and steel, serving as an essential undercoat before acid copper or further plating.

THE KEY NUMBER
Free Cyanide 1.0–3.0 oz/gal (7.5–22.5 g/L)
Free cyanide controls anode corrosion, deposit quality, and throwing power — too low causes rough, dark deposits; too high wastes cyanide and reduces efficiency.
WHAT YOU MUST DO
1
Analyze free cyanide, total cyanide, and copper metal at the start of every shift — keep the metal-to-free-cyanide ratio in the supplier's range
2
Maintain temperature at 140–160 °F (60–71 °C) for high-efficiency baths; 110–130 °F (43–54 °C) for strike baths
3
Plate at 10–30 ASF (1.1–3.2 A/dm²) — cyanide copper operates at lower current densities than acid copper
4
Maintain carbonate below 8 oz/gal (60 g/L) — excess carbonate reduces conductivity and causes rough deposits; freeze-out if needed
5
Use rolled or ball copper anodes in steel baskets with anode bags — maintain anode area at 2:1 anode-to-cathode ratio
COMMON PROBLEMS
Problem What to Look For What to Do
Dark or burned depositParts come out dark brown or black, especially at high-current areasCheck free cyanide (too low); reduce current density; verify temperature is at target; check for organic contamination
Poor adhesion on die castingsCopper coating peels or blisters during subsequent plating or testingReview the pre-plate cycle — die castings need a dedicated alkaline soak clean, acid pickle, and cyanide copper strike before the plate bath
Carbonate buildupBath becomes sluggish; conductivity drops; deposit becomes rough and dull; white crystals may appear at tank edgesAnalyze carbonate level — if above 8 oz/gal (60 g/L), remove by cooling the bath to 35 °F (2 °C) to crystallize and remove carbonate as sodium carbonate
⚠ SAFETY
●THIS BATH CONTAINS CYANIDE — a lethal poison; never mix with any acid; hydrogen cyanide gas (HCN) is released instantly on acid contact and is deadly
●Maintain bath pH above 10.5 at all times — lower pH liberates HCN gas; verify pH before every shift
●Cyanide-rated emergency equipment (amyl nitrite, emergency shower, eyewash) must be immediately accessible at all times
●Only trained and authorized personnel may work with cyanide baths — wear full PPE including face shield, nitrile gloves, and rubber apron; HCN detector must be operational
Consult the supervisor before adjusting process parameters.

Why Alkaline Zinc? vs acid zinc

AdvantageExplanation
Throwing powerCathode efficiency drops at high CD, redistributing metal into recesses. 1.5:1–2:1 ratio vs 3:1–5:1 for acid.
DuctilityDeposit bends and forms without cracking — critical for stamped and spring steel.
No cyanideEliminates cyanide waste treatment, reduces liability, meets modern regulatory standards.
Uniform passivateEven thickness = even chromate appearance. No “hot spots” at edges.
Lower H₂ riskLess H₂ evolved per unit zinc. Bake-out still required for HSLA >40 HRC.
Broad substratesPlates cast iron, HSLA, spring steel, and mixed-metal racks with fewer issues.

Troubleshooting Process-level

Poor Adhesion / Peeling

Inadequate cleaning or parts dried between stages. Re-evaluate soak clean; water break test; check transfer time.

Dark or Streaky Deposit

Iron or copper contamination. Analyze bath for metals; dummy at low CD; check anode condition.

Uneven Thickness

NaOH:Zn ratio out of range or poor rack contact. Titrate ratio; inspect rack tips and contacts.

White Haze on Passivate

Rinse drag-in to passivate tank or pH drift. Improve drain time; check passivate pH.

Blisters After Baking

H-embrittlement; bake-out late or too short. Bake within 4 hrs at 375±25°F for ≥8 hrs (ASTM B850).

Roughness

High carbonates or poor filtration. Analyze Na₂CO₃; decarbonate; check filter.

⚠ Safety Summary — All 8 Stages
Hot caustic (Stages 1, 5, 6) — chemical burns. Mineral acid (Stage 3) — burns and fumes. Chromium compounds (Stage 7) — irritant (Cr III) or carcinogen (Cr VI). Hydrogen gas (Stage 5) — flammable. Rectifier (Stage 5) — electrical/arc-flash. Emergency shower and eyewash within 10 seconds of every station. SDSs accessible. No eating, drinking, or smoking on the line.

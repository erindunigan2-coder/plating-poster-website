Key Procedures 7 steps

1
Verify bath chemistry before shift: NaOH, Zn, and ratio by titration. Log results.
2
Check temperature (70–90°F). Do not plate until corrected if out of range.
3
Set rectifier to correct amperage. Calculate surface area × target current density.
4
Load rack. All parts submerged with electrical contact. Verify cathode bar connection.
5
Plate for required time to achieve spec thickness. Verify with measurement.
6
Monitor V and A during plating. Sudden changes = contact problems or shorts.
7
Remove rack. Drain 15–20 sec (drag-out recovery). Transfer to post-plate rinse.

High CD = burn risk (edges, tips, corners)
Low CD = good coverage (recesses, interiors)
Alkaline zinc’s low cathode efficiency at high CD redistributes metal into recesses — this is its key advantage over acid zinc.

Do best

  • Titrate NaOH, Zn, and ratio every shift
  • Calculate CD for each load
  • Dummy at low CD when Fe >10 ppm (max 20 ppm)
  • Replenish with ZnO/NaOH only

Don’t avoid

  • Run on “how it looks”
  • Same rectifier setting for every load
  • Wait until deposits go dark to act
  • Add anything not in vendor TDS

Troubleshooting

Burning (Dark Edges)

CD high; Zn low; ratio low; temp high. Reduce amps; add ZnO; increase ratio.

Dull Deposit

Brightener depleted; temp low; organics. Add brightener; check temp; carbon-treat.

Pitting

H&sub2; gas adhesion; low carrier; poor agitation. Add carrier; increase agitation.

Poor Throwing Power

Ratio too low; carbonate high. Increase ratio to 15:1; decarbonate.

Roughness

Na&sub2;CO&sub3; >60 g/L; particulate; anode sludge. Decarbonate; check filter; bag anodes.

⚠ Safety — Hot Caustic + Electrical + H&sub2; Gas
NaOH (pH >13) causes severe chemical burns. H&sub2; gas at cathode — flammable/explosive in confined spaces. Rectifier: high amperage shock and arc-flash risk. PPE: goggles + face shield, elbow-length chemical gloves, apron, chemical boots. LOTO for rectifier work. Continuous ventilation over tank. No flames or sparks within 3 ft.

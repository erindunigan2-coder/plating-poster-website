ASTM B764 — STEP Test (Duplex Verification)
The point of a semi-bright base is corrosion performance, and that performance is only real if the layer potentials are right. The ASTM B764 STEP test measures the electrode potential of each nickel layer individually — confirming the bright layer is anodic (less noble) to the semi-bright base so it corrodes sacrificially. Right thicknesses, wrong potentials = a stack that fails corrosion testing.

The Duplex / Triplex Mechanism — Why This Stack Works
The reason to run semi-bright at all is the multilayer corrosion mechanism: a more-noble semi-bright base with a less-noble (sulfur-bearing) bright layer over it. When corrosion reaches the nickel, the bright layer corrodes preferentially and sideways instead of pitting straight down to the steel — dramatically extending life for the same total nickel thickness. Triplex adds a thin high-activity nickel and microdiscontinuous chrome to spread corrosion current over many tiny sites. All of it is governed by ASTM B456 and verified by the ASTM B764 STEP test (plus porosity/CASS testing per spec). Confirm the required layer thicknesses, thickness ratio, and potential differences against the current ASTM B456 and the customer specification — do not assume.

Final Rinse & Dry — Stage 8

  • DI water rinse, ambient, gentle immersion 15–30 sec
  • Warm air dry: 120–140°F max
  • No cure time for the nickel; topcoats/lacquers cure per their own TDS
  • Handle with clean, dry, lint-free gloves — fingerprints on fresh nickel can stain or interfere with the next layer’s adhesion
  • Re-activate the semi-bright before the bright layer if there was any delay — nickel passivates on standing and needs a light acid dip to restore adhesion before the next deposit
If a Chromium Topcoat Is Applied
The chrome process carries its own severe hazards. Hexavalent chromium (Cr⁶⁺) is a confirmed human carcinogen (IARC Group 1). OSHA PEL: 5 µg/m³ TWA (action level 2.5 µg/m³) as Cr(VI). Full PPE, mist suppressant, local exhaust, and medical surveillance per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1026 apply. This is the chrome line’s responsibility — see the Decorative Chrome / Trivalent Chrome references — not the nickel line’s. Keep chrome drag-back OUT of both nickel baths.

⚠ Safety
Bake ovens = burn and fire hazard — follow oven LOTO and hot-work procedures; no flammables near ovens. If bright nickel or a chromium topcoat is applied, their own hazards apply — nickel remains a skin sensitizer through handling (gloves at all times), and Cr(VI) requires full controls per OSHA 1910.1026 (see chrome references). Seal/lacquer chemistries: consult the specific product SDS.

⚠ Hydrogen Embrittlement Warning
The acid pickle is the single greatest source of hydrogen absorption in the acid zinc plating line. Uninhibited acid on high-strength steel (≥40 HRC / ≥180 ksi UTS) can load the lattice with atomic hydrogen in seconds. Use minimum effective acid concentration and dip time. Always use inhibitor for >40 HRC substrates. Consider alkaline zinc for embrittlement-sensitive applications.

Troubleshooting 6 common issues

Dark Oxide Remains

Acid too dilute or time too short. Increase concentration; extend time (check H-embrittlement risk).

Pitting / Etching

Too concentrated, too long, or too warm. Reduce concentration; shorten dip; add inhibitor.

Adhesion Failure Downstream

Incomplete activation; residual oxide. Re-evaluate acid strength and time; check for alkaline drag-in.

Excessive HCl Fuming

High concentration, high temp, or poor ventilation. Reduce to minimum effective; check exhaust system.

Dark Smut on Surface

High dissolved iron or copper contamination. Dump and recharge acid; investigate copper source.

H-Embrittlement Failures

Excessive pickle time; no inhibitor; >40 HRC steel. Shorten pickle; add inhibitor; review substrate hardness.

⚠ Safety — Strong Mineral Acid
HCl fumes at room temperature. H₂SO₄ causes severe dehydration burns. H₂ gas evolved during pickling is flammable. PPE: goggles + face shield, acid-resistant gloves (butyl/neoprene), apron, boots, respirator if ventilation is inadequate. Skin: flush 15 min. Eyes: eyewash 15 min. HCl inhalation: fresh air immediately. Spill: neutralize with soda ash. Do NOT use water alone on concentrated H₂SO₄.

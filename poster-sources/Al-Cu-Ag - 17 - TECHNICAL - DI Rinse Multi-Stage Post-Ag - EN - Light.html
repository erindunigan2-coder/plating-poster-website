Tighter Rinse Target Than Other Stages — DI Water Only
Final rinse conductivity must be below 20 µS/cm — tighter than the 50 µS/cm target used in earlier rinse stages. This stricter standard is required because residual cyanide on the silver surface causes tarnishing and staining in storage. Use DI water only — do NOT use tap water. Tap water leaves dried mineral residue (calcium/magnesium hardness, silica/scale) as white spots and water marks on the silver, and its dissolved solids raise final-rinse conductivity above target.

Silver Recovery Economics
Silver is a precious metal with significant commodity value. The first rinse tank captures the highest concentration of silver cyanide dragout from the plate bath. Here the silver is the very stable dicyanoargentate complex [Ag(CN)₂]− (log Kf ≈ 21), so adding NaCl will NOT precipitate AgCl — the cyanide keeps the silver bound and AgCl is itself soluble in cyanide. Recover the silver instead by electrowinning (silver cell), ion exchange, or evaporative recovery. Even small shops recover hundreds of dollars per month from this single tank. Do NOT dilute the recovery rinse with flowing water — keep it stagnant to maintain concentration. Never dump this tank to drain — you are literally pouring money away.

Why DI Water Only

Do NOT use tap water for any post-silver rinse stage. The dragout silver here is the dicyanoargentate complex [Ag(CN)₂]−, not free Ag+, so tap-water chloride does NOT precipitate AgCl on the part — and freshly plated metallic silver (Ag⁰) does not react with chloride under rinse conditions. The real problem is the dissolved minerals in tap water (Ca/Mg hardness, silica): they dry as white spots and water marks and raise final-rinse conductivity above the <20 µS/cm target. DI or high-purity RO water only.

Tap-water hardness & dissolved solids → white spotting + high conductivity. DI water only.

Effluent Routing

All rinse water beyond the recovery stage contains cyanide from silver plate dragout and must be routed to the cyanide destruct treatment system. Cyanide-bearing waste must never be mixed with acid waste streams. Verify your facility’s drain routing before operating. Residual silver in effluent should also be recovered or precipitated before discharge.

Effluent → cyanide destruct + silver recovery. NEVER to acid waste.

Troubleshooting Post-silver rinse issues

White Spots / Staining on Silver

Tap-water residue dried on the surface — mainly mineral hardness (Ca/Mg carbonates, silica/scale), not AgCl. Switch to DI water and avoid air-drying with tap water. Light spotting may wipe off; heavy staining requires re-cleaning or re-plating.

Rapid Silver Tarnishing

Residual cyanide from poor rinse quality. Final rinse conductivity too high. Improve rinse time and water quality. Target <20 µS/cm. Check DI system output.

Recovery Tank Overflowing

Excessive dragout volume. Ensure parts drain adequately over silver plate tank before transfer. Verify counterflow water is not leaking into recovery stage.

Anti-Tarnish Bath Not Working

Insufficient rinsing here carries cyanide into the anti-tarnish bath, contaminating it. Increase rinse time and check conductivity before allowing parts to proceed.

⚠ Safety
All rinse stages contain cyanide from silver plate dragout. Treat every tank in this rinse system as a cyanide-containing solution. Wear chemical-resistant gloves and face shield. Never route cyanide rinse water to acid waste drains — mixing cyanide with acid generates lethal HCN gas. All effluent must go to the cyanide destruct treatment system. Silver recovery solutions are also cyanide-bearing. Maintain ventilation. If you detect a bitter almond odor or HCN monitor alarms, evacuate immediately.

The Operating Window That Defines Passivation Quality
Target pH 1.8–2.2. A drift of just 0.3 units produces dramatic quality changes — too low dissolves the deposit, too high gives a thin, powdery film. Calibrate your meter daily with 1.68 and 4.01 buffers. pH strips are not acceptable. Trivalent passivation creates a chromium-based conversion coating that is RoHS-compliant. pH control is critical — even small drifts change color and protection level.

Key Procedures 7 steps

1
Verify pH with calibrated meter (NOT strips) — target 1.8–2.2. Calibrate daily with 1.68/4.01 buffers.
2
Check temp 20–35°C. Warmer = thicker film. Stay within range for consistent results.
3
Cr3+ concentration 10–20 g/L by titration. Below 10 g/L gives thin, poor-protection films.
4
Immerse 60–90 s with gentle agitation only. Vigorous agitation strips the forming film.
5
Do NOT rinse immediately — 10–15 s drain/drip. The film is still reacting and very soft.
6
Rinse with DI water <5 µS/cm, gentle spray or immersion. Hard water leaves deposits in the film.
7
Transfer to sealer or drying within 60 s — the film is soft and vulnerable to damage.

Do best

  • Calibrate pH meter daily with 1.68/4.01 buffers
  • Use DI water <5 µS/cm for post-passivation rinse
  • Track Zn/Fe contamination biweekly
  • Dedicate a separate DI rinse for post-passivation
  • Check passivation pH before every production run

Don’t avoid

  • Use pH strips — not accurate enough for this window
  • Air-agitate the passivation tank
  • Touch parts bare-handed after passivation
  • Let parts sit wet >5 min before sealer or drying
  • Carry over plating bath chemistry — it poisons the passivation

Troubleshooting

Film Too Thin / Low Salt Spray

pH too low (dissolving film), Cr3+ depleted, or temp too cold. Verify all three parameters and adjust.

Blotchy / Non-Uniform Color

Poor rinsing before passivation, alkaline drag-in, or Fe contamination. Check pre-rinse conductivity and Fe level.

Film Cracking / Peeling

Film too thick from excessive time or high temp. Reduce immersion time; check for pH drift above 2.5.

Rapid pH Drift

Excessive alkaline drag-in from poor rinsing. Tighten upstream rinse to <50 µS/cm; increase bath volume.

⚠ Safety — Chromium & Acids
Cr3+ (trivalent) is a mild irritant, not a carcinogen — wear chemical-resistant gloves and splash goggles when handling passivation solutions. Cr6+ (hexavalent), if legacy process, is an IARC Group 1 carcinogen — OSHA PEL 5 µg/m³; requires enclosed tanks, local exhaust, air monitoring per 29 CFR 1910.1026. HNO₃ for pH adjustment is highly corrosive — always add acid to water; use face shield and acid-resistant apron. NaF is toxic — avoid inhalation, store separately from acids to prevent HF release. Neutralize acid spills with soda ash; LOTO all agitation and pumps before tank maintenance. Eyewash and safety shower within 10 seconds per ANSI Z358.1.

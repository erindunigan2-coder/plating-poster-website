Low-P (2–4% P)

pH 8.5–9.5 · alkaline bath
  • Microcrystalline structure
  • Ferromagnetic deposit
  • 650–750 HV as-plated
  • 1000–1100 HV after heat treat
  • Rate: 10–15 µm/hr
  • Excellent solderability
Most common

Mid-P (5–9% P)

pH 4.6–5.2 · acid bath
  • Mixed amorphous / crystalline
  • 500–600 HV as-plated
  • Rate: 18–25 µm/hr (fastest)
  • ENIG standard for PCB
  • General-purpose coating
  • Most widely used class
Corrosion king

High-P (10–13% P)

pH 4.2–4.8 · acid bath
  • Fully amorphous structure
  • Non-magnetic deposit
  • 450–550 HV as-plated
  • 1000+ hr salt spray (SST)
  • Maximum corrosion resistance
  • Electronics / HDD applications

Common Problems Diagnose & correct

Skip Plating

Contamination or poor activation. Improve cleaning protocol; verify activation; run a water-break test.

Pitting

Chloride drag-in from HCl activation, or particulate. Better DI rinse pre-plate; filter bath 5–10 µm continuously.

Bath Decomposition

Low stabilizer or idling at operating temp. Check stabilizer ppm; never leave bath hot without parts or a dummy.

Low Deposition Rate

pH drift, low temp, or reducer depleted. Check pH every 2–4 hr; verify temp; replenish hypophosphite.

Technical reference only. Values shown are typical operating ranges — consult your chemistry supplier for bath-specific parameters. See ASTM B733 for plating specification requirements.

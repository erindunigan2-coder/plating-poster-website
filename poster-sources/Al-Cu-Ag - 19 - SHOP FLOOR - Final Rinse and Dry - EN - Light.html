Warm Air Dry — No Shop Air
Dry your parts with warm air at 104–131°F. Use the dedicated dryer only — do NOT use shop air or a regular air hose. Shop air has chemicals in it that will turn the silver dark. Put on fresh clean gloves before touching any dried parts. No bare hands from here on out.
What You Must Do
1
Final rinse in clean DI water. If the anti-tarnish dip was used, keep it to a brief, gentle drag-out dip — do NOT scrub or over-rinse, because that film is supposed to stay on the silver to protect it. If the anti-tarnish dip was skipped, give the parts a normal thorough DI rinse.
2
Dry with warm air (104–131°F). Use the dedicated dryer only. Make sure all water is gone from every surface, including bolt holes and recesses.
3
Put on CLEAN lint-free gloves. Do not reuse your plating gloves. Get a fresh pair. No bare-hand contact with the silver surface — fingerprints are permanent.
4
Package immediately in plastic bags. Use sulfur-free polyethylene bags. Seal the bags. Do not use cardboard, rubber bands, or newspaper — these will tarnish the silver.
✓ Correct
  • Warm air from dedicated dryer only
  • Clean lint-free gloves for all handling
  • Parts fully dry before packaging
  • Sealed in sulfur-free PE bags
✗ Wrong
  • Shop air or compressed air used to dry
  • Bare hands touched the silver surface
  • Parts packed in cardboard or newspaper
  • Parts left on bench exposed to air
Common Problems
What You SeeLikely CauseWhat To Do
Fingerprints on silverSomeone touched parts without clean glovesParts need re-plating. Fingerprints are permanent. Tell your supervisor.
Water spots after dryingDryer not hot enough or parts not fully dryCheck dryer temperature. Make sure all surfaces are completely dry.
Silver turning dark in storageBad packaging or sulfur in the airCheck packaging material. Must be sulfur-free PE bags, sealed. Tell your supervisor.
Part dropped or scratchedHandling errorPart is likely scrap. Tell your supervisor. Handle carefully — these parts took the full process to make.
⚠ Safety
Hot air burn risk from the dryer (104–131°F). Be careful when positioning parts. These busbars are heavy — dropping one can injure your feet or hands. Wear your safety shoes. Handle plated parts with care — a dropped busbar after the full plating process is expensive scrap.

This is the last step. Inspect parts, package in sealed plastic bags, and send to shipping.

Stage A: Soak Clean → Stage B: Electroclean
Two cleaning stages in series. Soak dissolves bulk oils by heat and alkalinity. Electroclean scrubs residual films with gas evolution at the part surface. Both must pass a water-break test before proceeding.

Key Procedures — 7 Steps
1
Verify soak tank temp 60–75°C and alkalinity 60–80 g/L NaOH by titration.
2
Immerse 5–15 min; skim floating oil at start of shift.
3
Water-break test after soak; if fail, extend time or replenish cleaner.
4
Electroclean: cathodic 30–60 s then anodic 15–30 s — MUST finish on anodic.
5
Current density 5–10 A/dm²; voltage 4–8 V.
6
Water-break test after electroclean — must hold 30 s unbroken.
7
Transfer to acid activation within 60 s — do not allow parts to dry.
Troubleshooting
Water-Break Failure

Oil contamination or low alkalinity. Check NaOH; skim surface; extend immersion; add surfactant.

Uneven Gas Evolution

Poor electrical contact or passivated rack tips. Clean and re-tin contacts; verify rectifier.

Dark Smut After Electroclean

Finishing on cathodic cycle deposits metallic smut. Always end on anodic.

Skip-Plate in Acid Bath

Residual organics — acid Zn-Ni has no stripping action. Return to soak clean; verify water-break.

⚠ Safety — Caustic + Current + Heat
NaOH causes severe chemical burns — immediate tissue damage at working concentrations. H₂ gas generated at cathode — ensure ventilation >1 m/s face velocity over tank; explosive at 4–75% in air. Tanks operate at 65°C — thermal burn hazard on splash. Rectifier lockout/tagout before any rack or anode maintenance. PPE: splash goggles, face shield, nitrile/neoprene gloves, apron.
Operator Checklist
✓ Water-break test every rack
✓ Skim oil every shift
✓ Final cycle anodic — always
✓ Rinse conductivity <200 µS/cm

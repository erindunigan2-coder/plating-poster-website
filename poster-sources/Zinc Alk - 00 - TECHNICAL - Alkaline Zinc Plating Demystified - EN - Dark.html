Typical Deposit Thickness (µm) — Rack & Barrel Alkaline Zinc
Alkaline non-cyanide zinc produces a matte-to-bright deposit with excellent throwing power into recesses and barrel loads. At 8–25 µm with trivalent passivation, alkaline zinc routinely delivers 120–500+ hours of white corrosion in salt spray depending on thickness and sealant.

Why Alkaline Zinc Dominates Industrial Finishing
Alkaline zinc plating replaced toxic cyanide zinc in most facilities by the 1990s while keeping its best trait: outstanding throwing power. The high-pH bath (NaOH-based) distributes zinc uniformly into deep recesses, threads, and barrel loads where acid zinc struggles. It generates less hydrogen embrittlement risk than acid baths, making it preferred for hardened fasteners (Class 10.9+). Combined with trivalent passivation and a topcoat sealer, alkaline zinc is the go-to corrosion barrier for automotive fasteners, brackets, stampings, and general industrial hardware.

Did You Know?
Alkaline zinc plating was developed in the 1960s as a direct replacement for toxic cyanide zinc baths. The breakthrough was using sodium hydroxide as the complexing agent instead of sodium cyanide, achieving nearly identical throwing power with dramatically lower toxicity. Today, alkaline non-cyanide zinc accounts for over 60% of all zinc plating in North America. The process is so forgiving that many job shops run the same bath chemistry for decades with only minor adjustments, making it one of the most “set-and-forget” plating processes in the industry.

Alkaline Zinc vs Acid Zinc when to use each

Alkaline Zinc Choose When

  • Deep recesses, threads, barrel loads
  • Hardened steel (reduced HE risk)
  • Thick deposits needed (>12 µm)
  • Broad current density tolerance
  • Simple waste treatment required

Acid Zinc Choose Instead

  • Bright decorative finish required
  • Die castings or zinc substrates
  • Thinner deposits with high leveling
  • Faster plating speed needed
  • Room-temperature operation preferred
⚠ Safety Snapshot
Sodium hydroxide (NaOH 120–150 g/L) — strongly caustic; causes severe skin and eye burns on contact and forms caustic mist and heat when mixing. Federal OSHA PEL 2 mg/m³. Always add caustic to water, never the reverse; full face shield, chemical goggles, gauntlet gloves, and apron required. Hydrogen gas evolves at the cathode — no open flames or sparks; ventilate the tank. Chelators / brighteners complicate waste treatment — segregate and treat to permit limits. Emergency shower and eyewash within reach; current SDS at every station. No eating, drinking, or smoking on the line.

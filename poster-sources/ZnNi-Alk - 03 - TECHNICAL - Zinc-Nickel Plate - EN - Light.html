Depositing the 12–15% Nickel Alloy — The Heart of the Process

Controlled co-deposition of two metals in a single bath — where chemistry, current, and temperature must all agree.

12–15%
Ni by Weight in Deposit
The gamma-phase Zn-Ni alloy forms only within this narrow window. Below 12% the deposit loses its noble character; above 15% the phase shifts to alpha and corrosion performance drops sharply. Alkaline Zn-Ni has superior throwing power vs acid. Bath Zn:Ni metal ratio is ~6.5:1. Carbonate buildup above 50 g/L reduces throwing power — control by cooling or decanting.

Key Procedures 7 steps

1
Verify bath: Zn 6–10 g/L, Ni 0.8–1.5 g/L, NaOH 120–150 g/L.
2
Check temp 22–30°C (chiller if needed; >32°C shifts Ni% up).
3
Set CD: 1–5 A/dm² rack, 0.5–1.5 A/dm² barrel.
4
Run Hull cell: 2 A, 10 min, 25°C — evaluate brightness + alloy by XRF.
5
Plate to target thickness (10 µm ≈ 25–30 min at 2.5 A/dm², ~55% cathode efficiency).
6
Monitor additives per supplier schedule (every 500–1,000 A·h/L).
7
Carbon treat every 2–4 weeks; filtration 2–4 turnovers/h through 5 µm.

Do best

  • Hull cell every shift
  • Maintain Zn:Ni ratio 6.5–7:1
  • Monitor carbonate monthly
  • Log Ni%, thickness, temp every load
  • Run Hull cell test at least once per shift for alloy verification

Don’t avoid

  • Adjust Zn and Ni simultaneously
  • Exceed 32°C bath temp
  • Neglect carbon treatment schedule
  • Plate without verifying rinse cleanliness
  • Plate without verifying Ni% is within 12–15% range

Troubleshooting

Ni% Below 12%

Low Ni metal; high CD; low temp. Add Ni concentrate; reduce CD; verify temp >22°C. Run Hull cell to confirm.

Ni% Above 15%

Excess Ni; low CD; high temp. Reduce Ni additions; increase CD; cool bath below 30°C.

Dull / Hazy / Streaky Deposits

Organic contamination or depleted brightener. Carbon treat immediately; replenish additives per TDS.

Poor Throwing Power

Low NaOH or high carbonate. Titrate and adjust NaOH; freeze-out carbonate if >50 g/L.

High Carbonate (>50 g/L)

CO2 absorption from air into caustic bath. Reduce by cooling to precipitate Na2CO3; maintain tank lids. High carbonate reduces conductivity and wastes current.

Blistering / Peeling

Inadequate cleaning or activation before plating. Review pretreatment; check water-break test compliance; verify acid activation concentration and time.

⚠ Safety — Caustic + Nickel + Hydrogen
NaOH at pH 14 — severe corrosive; causes deep chemical burns on contact. Nickel compounds are Category 1A carcinogens by inhalation (IARC Group 1). Airborne Ni must remain <0.1 mg/m³ (ACGIH TLV). Hydrogen gas accounts for 40–50% of cathode current — explosive in air at 4–75%; continuous ventilation required. Nickel in wastewater must be treated to <1 mg/L before discharge. PPE: chemical goggles, face shield, nitrile/neoprene gloves, apron. Never allow H₂ to accumulate in enclosed spaces above the tank.

#1
You Can’t Control What You Don’t Measure
Thickness verification is the single most fundamental quality check in any plating operation. Every customer specification, every ASTM standard, and every functional performance requirement ultimately comes down to “did you deposit enough material, in the right place?” Without reliable measurement, you’re guessing.

Method Highlights Key advantages & calibration

💡
XRF Advantages

Non-destructive multi-layer analysis in seconds. Resolves individual layer thicknesses in complex stacks (e.g., Cr/Ni/Cu on steel). Works on virtually any substrate. The industry workhorse for production QC.

🔬
Cross-Section Value

Visual confirmation of actual deposit structure. Reveals porosity, lamination, nodules, and interface quality that no non-destructive method can detect. The ultimate referee method per ASTM B487.

⚙
Calibration Requirements

Every method requires traceable standards matched to your coating/substrate combination. Certified foils or electroformed standards per NIST traceability. Recalibrate per shift or after 50 readings minimum.

Best Practices Do

  • Calibrate with standards matching your exact coating/substrate system
  • Measure at specified significant surfaces per the customer print
  • Take multiple readings and report the average with min/max
  • Clean the probe tip and part surface before each measurement
  • Use cross-section to verify XRF on new part geometries

Common Mistakes Avoid

  • Using magnetic gauge on non-magnetic substrates (wrong physics)
  • Measuring on curved surfaces without a correction factor or fixture
  • Accepting XRF readings near edges where X-ray spot overlaps air
  • Calibrating once per week instead of per shift or daily minimum
  • Ignoring surface roughness effects — Ra > 1 µm degrades accuracy
XRF vs. Cross-Section — Workhorse vs. Referee
XRF is the industry workhorse because it’s fast, non-destructive, and resolves multi-layer stacks. But XRF averages over a spot size (typically 0.1–3 mm diameter) and can be fooled by alloy composition variations, rough surfaces, or thin layers near its detection limit. A polished cross-section shows exactly what’s there — every layer, every interface, every defect. When XRF and cross-section disagree, the cross-section wins. That’s why most customer specs and ASTM standards designate microscopic cross-section as the referee method for disputes.

Measurement Problems Common sources of error

Curved Surface Error

Probe-to-surface coupling changes on radii. Use V-block fixtures or curved calibration standards. XRF spot may partially miss the part on tight radii < 5 mm.

Multi-Layer Interference

XRF fluorescence peaks overlap when adjacent layers have similar atomic numbers (e.g., Fe/Ni/Co). Requires matrix correction or fundamental parameters software calibration.

Calibration Drift

X-ray tube aging, probe wear, and temperature changes cause systematic drift. Run a check standard every 25–50 readings. Recalibrate if readings shift > 5% from known value.

Operator Technique

Inconsistent probe pressure, angle, or placement causes scatter. Standardize measurement locations on parts. Use fixtures to eliminate human variables on critical dimensions.

⚠ Safety — Measurement Equipment Hazards
XRF instruments contain X-ray tubes or radioactive sources — never bypass shutter interlocks, maintain radiation safety training, and follow your facility’s radiation protection program. Portable units require registration with state radiation control. Coulometric solutions contain concentrated acids or electrolytes (HCl, H₂SO₄) — wear chemical splash goggles and gloves. Metallographic preparation uses grinding/polishing media and chemical etchants (nital, acidic solutions) — use fume hood for etching, eye protection for cutting and grinding.

Best Practices Critical Guidelines

✓ Do

  • Record time out of chrome — clock is running
  • Pre-heat oven to 375 °F before parts arrive
  • Attach recording thermocouple to load
  • Allow parts to cool in oven or still air — no quench

✗ Don’t

  • Delay bake — HE risk increases every minute past the window
  • Open oven during bake cycle unless required for monitoring
  • Quench parts after bake — thermal shock cracks chrome
  • Bake dripping-wet parts — dry with compressed air first

Oven Loading & Thermocouple Placement Operational Detail

Loading Best Practices

  • Space parts for uniform air circulation — do not stack or nest
  • Use wire racks or open fixtures; avoid solid trays that block airflow
  • Heavy loads can drop oven temp 50–100°F on insertion — allow recovery
  • Blow off residual rinse water with clean compressed air before loading
  • Mixed-hardness loads: use the most restrictive bake window and duration

Thermocouple Protocol

  • Attach contact TC to the heaviest part — it reaches temperature last
  • Place probe on part surface, not in air stream (air vs. part temp can differ 30°F+)
  • Bake time begins when the PART reads 375°F, not when the oven setpoint recovers
  • Use recording (chart or digital) thermocouple — printout becomes part of the quality record
  • Calibrate TCs per facility schedule; out-of-cal TC = invalid bake record
Hydrogen Diffusion Metallurgy
Atomic hydrogen absorbed during cathodic plating diffuses to grain boundaries, voids, and stress concentrations, where it recombines to molecular H2, generating extreme localized pressure that initiates intergranular cracking. At 375°F the diffusion coefficient of hydrogen in ferritic steel increases by roughly 100× versus room temperature, driving hydrogen out through the surface before it accumulates at damage sites. The bake-within window is critical because hydrogen migration to traps is time-dependent — once hydrogen reaches a crack tip, baking cannot reverse the damage. Higher-hardness steels have more trapping sites and lower fracture toughness, which is why they require faster transfer to the oven and longer bake durations. Post-bake hardness may drop 50–100 HV from the as-plated condition due to mild tempering of the deposit microstructure.
⚠ Safety — Stage 9
Oven at 375 °F (190 °C) — contact burns. Heat-resistant gloves rated 400 °F+ for loading/unloading. Hot parts retain heat 30-60 min after removal — do not handle bare-handed. If parts not fully rinsed, trace Cr(VI) can volatilize in oven exhaust — ensure oven is vented. Do not use chrome bake oven for other materials. Keep area clear of flammable materials.

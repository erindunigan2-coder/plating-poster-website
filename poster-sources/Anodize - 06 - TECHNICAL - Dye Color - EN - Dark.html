Target pH — The Color Sweet Spot
Organic dyes absorb best at pH 5.0–6.0. Too low (acidic): dye attacks the oxide, colors fade. Too high (alkaline): poor absorption, pale results. Monitor with a calibrated pH meter — not strips. Dye concentration, temperature, and immersion time must be repeatable batch to batch. Run a test coupon before every production batch. Color matching must be done under controlled lighting — fluorescent vs. LED can shift perceived color significantly.

Key Procedures 6 steps

1
Verify dye bath: pH (5.0–6.0), temperature (130–145°F), and dye concentration before immersion.
2
Transfer from rinse immediately. Every minute of delay reduces dye uptake as pores begin to hydrate.
3
Immerse and agitate gently. Avoid trapping air bubbles — they leave undyed spots.
4
Hold for required time (5–30 min depending on depth of color). Light colors: short. Black: full time.
5
Check color against standard (wet — color shifts slightly when dry and sealed).
6
Rinse briefly in clean DI water, then proceed immediately to seal.

Troubleshooting

Pale / Weak Color

Low concentration; short time; pH too high; oxide too thin. Increase dye conc.; extend time; check anodize thickness.

Uneven / Blotchy Color

Poor rinse before dye; air entrapment; temp gradient in tank. Improve rinse; degas parts; add heater circulation.

Color Fades After Seal

Pores partially closed before dye (delayed transfer) or seal pH too low (acid leaches dye). Speed transfer; check seal pH.

Wrong Shade / Off-Color

pH drift; contamination (metals, other dyes); wrong concentration. Check pH; filter; make fresh dye if cross-contaminated.

Do best

  • Monitor pH with calibrated meter every batch
  • Keep dye tanks covered when not in use
  • Use DI water for all dye makeup
  • Color-match wet against a sealed standard
  • Run a test coupon before each production batch for color verification

Don’t avoid

  • Mix different dye brands in the same tank
  • Let parts air-dry before dyeing
  • Use pH strips instead of a meter
  • Skip rinse between dye and seal
  • Mix dye brands or chemistries without supplier approval
⚠ Safety — Hot Dye Solution + pH Adjusters
Dye tanks operate at 130–145°F — burn risk from hot liquid and steam. Acetic acid (pH down) is a mild acid but causes eye irritation. Ammonium hydroxide (pH up) releases ammonia fumes — use with ventilation. Dyes stain skin and clothing permanently. PPE: goggles, chemical gloves, apron. Ventilation required when adjusting pH. This step applies to Type II decorative anodizing. Type III hard anodize lines typically bypass this tank and proceed directly to seal. Some dye formulations contain sensitizers. Avoid skin contact and use local exhaust ventilation. Report any allergic reactions to your supervisor. Some organic dyes contain sensitizing agents that can cause occupational dermatitis with repeated exposure. Maintain a dye SDS binder at the station and review it when introducing new colors.

Process Stages — Clean → Rinse → Activate → Rinse → Plate → Rinse → Post-Treat → Rinse / Dry
Plain Watts nickel is the engineering counterpart to bright nickel — same core chemistry, none of the organic brightener system. The deposit is matte to semi-lustrous, ductile, and sulfur-free. Every upstream stage either protects the bath or poisons it. There is no neutral step.
Critical Control Points WATCH THESE 3
01 — Cleaning
No leveling and no brightener — every prep miss plates through in plain sight. Water-break clean or stop the rack.
04 — Guard Rinse
< 200 µS/cm. The Watts bath is maintained for years, never dumped — whatever gets past this rinse stays in the bath.
05 — Plating
Metal, pH, temperature, current — four dials. Burning means pH too high or boric acid / nickel metal low — not “add more amps.”

Plain Watts is NOT Bright Nickel Same core bath — the brightener package is the only difference
  • It is NOT full mirror bright — the deposit is matte to semi-lustrous, by design.
  • It has NO brightener system — no carrier, no primary brightener, no leveler. The only organic addition is the anti-pit wetter.
  • It does NOT co-deposit sulfur — the deposit stays safe for brazing, soldering, and thermal service.
⚠  Do NOT try to “fix” a matte deposit by adding brightener.
Spec tip — ASTM B689 Type 1 (use current revision) = nickel plated with no brighteners, hardeners, or stress additives: exactly this bath. Bake high-strength steel per ASTM B850.
⚠ Line Safety
Hot alkaline cleaners, strong mineral acids, and hot acidic nickel solution — nickel salts are skin sensitizers and nickel compounds are classified as suspected carcinogens. OSHA PEL, soluble nickel: 1 mg/m³ TWA (as Ni). Nickel itch is cumulative and permanent once sensitized — gloves whenever handling parts, racks, or wet work. Rectifiers = high amperage: LOTO before reaching into any tank. Each stage poster carries its own full safety panel.

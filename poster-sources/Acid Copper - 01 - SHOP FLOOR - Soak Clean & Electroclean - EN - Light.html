Water-Break Time — Clean = Continuous, Unbroken Water Sheet
A clean surface holds an unbroken film of water. If water beads or breaks, organic soil remains — and it will show up downstream as pitting, skip plating, or adhesion loss under the bright copper. The test is free and non-negotiable.
The Water-Break Test — Pass vs Fail DO IT AFTER EVERY CLEAN
✗ Fail — Water Beads
Organic soil remains. Water retracts into beads. Result: pitting · skip plating · adhesion loss. Re-clean — do not proceed.
✓ Pass — Sheets Off
Clean surface. Water holds a continuous, unbroken sheet across the whole part. Good to proceed to rinse and activation.

✓ Do
  • Run the water-break test after cleaning — every load
  • Soak first, then electroclean steel & zinc die cast headed for a strike
  • Finish electroclean anodic (15–30s) to strip metallic smut
  • Handle cleaned parts with clean gloves only
✗ Don’t
  • Proceed on a part that fails the water break
  • Run zinc die cast too hot / too long — it etches
  • Over-cathodic zinc die cast — hydrogen blistering risk
  • Reach into a live electroclean tank — LOTO first
⚠ Safety
Hot alkaline solution — chemical burns on contact. Electrocleaning generates hydrogen and oxygen — ventilation required, no open flames near tanks. Goggles, face shield, elbow-length chemical gloves, apron, chemical-resistant boots. Skin/eye contact: flush 15 minutes. The electrocleaner carries DC current in solution — LOTO before reaching into the tank.
Operator reference only. Follow your facility SOPs. Verify parameters against your chemistry supplier’s TDS. Report any process deviations to your supervisor.

Oxide Removal — Exposing Bare Metal Before Plating

Even a freshly cleaned part re-oxidizes within seconds in air. Acid activation strips that invisible oxide film so the first plating layer bonds directly to bare metal. The right acid, concentration, and time depend entirely on the substrate.

Typical Activation Dwell — Enough to Remove Oxide, Not Enough to Etch
Acid activation is a controlled dissolution of the surface oxide layer. Too short and oxide remains — causing skip-plate. Too long and the acid attacks the base metal — causing roughness, pitting, or dimensional loss. Steel uses HCl; zinc die-cast needs fluoride-bearing acids; aluminum requires specialized zincate or nitric-HF sequences.

The Balance: Remove Oxide Without Attacking Base Metal
Acid activation is a race between oxide dissolution (wanted) and base metal etching (unwanted). Steel tolerates HCl well but prolonged exposure causes hydrogen absorption and roughness. Zinc die-cast is extremely sensitive — even 10 seconds too long in fluoride acid can cause intergranular attack and porosity that no amount of plating will fix. Aluminum re-oxidizes within seconds of leaving acid, so transfer to the next station must be immediate and parts must stay wet.

Activation Troubleshooting common failures

Good Practice Do

  • Match acid type and concentration to substrate
  • Keep acid fresh — dissolved metals reduce effectiveness
  • Rinse immediately after activation — no air dry
  • Time with stopwatch, not by feel

Common Mistakes Avoid

  • Using same acid for all substrates
  • Leaving parts in acid during breaks or downtime
  • Letting activated parts air-dry (re-oxidizes instantly)
  • Ignoring acid temperature (too hot = aggressive etch)
⚠ Safety Summary — Acid Activation
HCl — corrosive; releases HCl fumes; OSHA PEL 5 ppm (ceiling); local exhaust ventilation mandatory. HF (fluoride acids) — extremely dangerous; penetrates skin causing deep tissue burns and systemic fluoride poisoning; calcium gluconate gel must be on-site. H₂SO₄ — severe burns; exothermic on dilution. HNO₃ — oxidizer; reacts violently with organics. Wear face shield, chemical-resistant gloves, and acid-resistant apron. Emergency shower/eyewash within 10 sec. SDSs posted.

Diffusion & Heat — Induction Harden
INDUCTION HARDEN
Localized surface hardening using electromagnetic induction
Induction hardening heats and quenches specific areas in seconds — coil design and power settings must match the part geometry exactly.

THE KEY NUMBER
850–1000 °C (1560–1830 °F) surface temperature, 1–10 second heat time
Power, frequency, and heat time control how deep and how hot the heat pattern goes — even a half-second change affects case depth.
WHAT YOU MUST DO
1
Verify the correct induction coil is installed for this part number — wrong coil = wrong pattern
2
Set power (kW), frequency (kHz), heat time, and quench delay per the recipe card — do not modify settings without engineering approval
3
Run the first piece and section it for hardness profile — confirm case depth and pattern before running production
4
Monitor quench flow rate and concentration (polymer quench) — insufficient quench causes soft spots
5
Check parts for cracks after hardening — magnetic particle inspect or dye penetrant per the spec
COMMON PROBLEMS
Problem What to Look For What to Do
Soft spotsHardness readings below spec in localized areas of the patternCheck coil-to-part gap (coupling) — uneven gap causes uneven heating; also verify quench spray coverage
CrackingCracks visible after hardening, often at stress risers, keyways, or diameter transitionsReduce power, increase heat time (slower heating), or add a quench delay — temper parts immediately; check material for inclusions
Through-hardening (case too deep)Hardness extends through the full cross-section instead of just the surfacePart is too small for the power level or heat time is too long — reduce power or use a higher frequency to keep heat shallow
⚠ SAFETY
●Induction coils carry high current and high voltage — never touch the coil, workpiece, or connections during operation
●Parts reach 1000 °C in seconds — do not handle without heat-resistant tongs; parts remain hot after quench
●Quench spray creates steam and mist — wear face shield and stay clear during the quench cycle
●Magnetic fields near the coil can affect pacemakers and medical implants — maintain the posted exclusion zone
Consult the supervisor before adjusting process parameters.

3 ROLES
What This Copper Does Next
Acid copper is usually an undercoat (on to bright nickel + chrome), a functional/PCB deposit (verified and finished), or a decorative bare-copper finish (which must be protected, because copper tarnishes fast). Know which role applies before you leave this stage.
What Is This Copper? — Route It PICK ONE PATH
Undercoat →
Bright Nickel + Chrome
The decorative triad. Activate the copper first, then proceed to nickel — copper’s leveling carries the whole system.
Bare copper →
Protect It
Anti-tarnish (benzotriazole / chromate), clear lacquer, or antique blackening. Copper oxidizes fast — don’t leave it bare.
Functional / PCB →
QC + Finish
Verify thickness / adhesion, then move to imaging / etch or a final finish (ENIG / OSP) per process.
⚠ H-Embrittlement
Side Gate
Hardened steel >40 HRC: bake 375 ±25°F for ≥8 h, starting within 4 h of the last electrodeposition step (ASTM B850). Verify per customer spec whether the bake comes before nickel/chrome.

Final rinse & dry: DI rinse, warm-air dry 120–140°F max, then protect bright copper. Handle with clean lint-free gloves — fingerprints etch permanently into fresh copper.
✓ Do
  • Decide the copper’s role before it leaves the stage
  • Activate copper before nickel; move fast, keep it wet
  • Protect bare copper immediately — anti-tarnish or lacquer
  • Bake hardened steel on the 4-hour clock (ASTM B850)
✗ Don’t
  • Let fresh copper oxidize before nickel — it hazes
  • Handle bright copper bare-handed
  • Miss the 4-hour bake window on >40 HRC parts
  • Ship bare copper unprotected — it tarnishes fast
⚠ Safety
Depends on the path. Anti-tarnish / blackening chemistries vary — some contain selenium or sulfide compounds; consult the SDS. If a hexavalent chromate is used, Cr⁶ is a confirmed carcinogen (OSHA PEL 5 µg/m³) — full respiratory protection and controls apply. Lacquer solvents are flammable — ventilation required. Standard chemical PPE throughout.

Two-Stage Clean — Soak Then Scrub
Stage A (soak clean) uses heat and alkalinity to dissolve bulk oils and greases. Stage B (electroclean) generates gas at the part surface to lift residual films. Both stages must pass the water-break test before the part moves on. Alkaline zinc-nickel is even more sensitive to surface contamination than acid baths. Thorough cleaning is non-negotiable.
Operator Procedures
1
Verify soak tank temp 60–75°C and alkalinity by titration before loading parts.
2
Immerse parts 5–10 min. Skim floating oil from the surface at start of every shift.
3
Water-break test after soak. If water sheets evenly for 30 s, transfer to electroclean.
4
Electroclean: cathodic 30–60 s, then anodic 15–30 s. Always finish on anodic to strip smut.
5
Water-break test after electroclean — must hold 30 s unbroken. Fail = re-clean.
6
Transfer to acid activation within 60 s. Do not allow parts to dry between tanks.

⚠ Safety — Alkaline Cleaners + Electrical Hazards
NaOH causes severe chemical burns at working concentrations — immediate tissue damage on contact. Hydrogen gas generated at cathode — ensure ventilation >1 m/s face velocity; explosive at 4–75% in air. Tanks at 65°C — thermal burn hazard on splash. Rectifier lockout/tagout required before any rack or anode work. PPE: splash goggles, face shield, nitrile/neoprene gloves, apron. Alkaline cleaners cause skin irritation and eye damage on contact. Flush with water for 15+ minutes if splashed. Keep eyewash accessible.

23 Hours — Standard HE Bake for Steel >1,050 MPa
Per ASTM B850, parts with UTS >1,050 MPa (>39 HRC) must be baked at 191°C ±14°C. The clock starts when the PART reaches temperature — not when the oven turns on. Begin bake within 4 hours of plating (1–2 h preferred). Alkaline Zn-Ni generates ~45% hydrogen at the cathode vs ~5% for acid, making HE bake especially critical. There is no safe amount of hydrogen in steels above 39 HRC.

Key Procedures 7 steps

1
Determine if bake is required: UTS >1,050 MPa (>39 HRC) = YES. Check drawing or spec sheet.
2
Start bake within 4 hours of plating (1–2 h preferred). Delay increases HE risk exponentially.
3
Load parts — do NOT stack. Every surface must be exposed to oven air for uniform hydrogen diffusion.
4
Thermocouple ON THE PART — time starts when the part reaches 191°C, not the oven setpoint.
5
Bake times: 39–48 HRC = 14 h min; >48 HRC = 23 h min + ASTM F519 testing required.
6
Cool to <50°C before handling. Do NOT quench — thermal shock can crack high-strength steel.
7
Final inspection: XRF thickness (3–5 locations), XRF alloy (12–15% Ni), adhesion (ASTM B571), appearance, salt spray panels, friction (fasteners).

Do best

  • Part-mounted thermocouple every load
  • Maintain a bake log with part ID, time, temp
  • Oven TUS quarterly per AMS 2750
  • Label baked parts immediately after cooling

Don’t avoid

  • Assume bake fixes bad plating (it doesn’t)
  • Exceed 220°C — damages passivation film
  • Start the clock when the oven turns on
  • Mix bake-required and no-bake parts in same load

Troubleshooting

Coating Peels in Bend Test

Poor adhesion from contamination before plating, insufficient activation, or excessive thickness. Review pretreatment and plating parameters.

Parts Crack Days/Weeks Later

Hydrogen embrittlement. Bake was late, too short, or too cool. Audit bake log; verify thermocouple placement; increase bake time.

Ni% Out of Spec on Final

Alloy composition drifted during plating. Check bath chemistry, current density, and temperature. Ni <12% = poor corrosion protection.

Salt Spray Failure

Thin deposit, low Ni%, poor passivation, or missing sealer. Systematic check: thickness → alloy → passivation → sealer → test panels.

⚠ Safety — Ovens & Inspection
Oven at 191–220°C — severe burn risk. Heat-resistant gloves and face shield required when loading/unloading. Establish a cool-down staging area — parts remain dangerously hot for 30+ minutes after removal. XRF analyzers use X-ray radiation — follow manufacturer safety protocols; never point at people. H₂ off-gassing during bake — ensure oven ventilation; hydrogen is flammable at 4–75% concentration in air.

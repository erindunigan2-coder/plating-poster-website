Sulfur in Deposit — The Whole Duplex Mechanism
Keeping sulfur out of the deposit is what makes this base layer more noble than the bright nickel plated over it — so the bright layer corrodes sacrificially and the stack outlasts a single nickel layer of the same thickness (verified by the ASTM B764 STEP test). Add sulfur — wrong additive, bright-bath drag-over, or anode residues where a sulfur-free system is specified — and you lose the whole mechanism.

✓ Do
  • Dose the leveler on amp-hours — it’s consumed, not eternal
  • Carbon treat on a set Ah schedule — breakdown products = dull gray the bright layer can’t cover
  • Judge Hull panels on leveling and soundness — satin is correct here
  • Hold pH with H₂SO₄ down, NiCO₃ up — never NaOH
✗ Don’t
  • Dose bright-nickel (sulfur-bearing) brightener into this tank — ever. It kills the corrosion protection of the whole stack
  • Share pumps, cups, or dosing lines with the bright bath
  • Chase burning with more current — it’s chemistry, not amps
  • Expect mirror bright — the bright layer above does the shine
⚠ Safety
Hot acidic nickel solution (pH 3.5–4.5, up to 150°F) — burns, and a dermal sensitizer. Nickel salts are skin sensitizers; nickel compounds are classified as suspected carcinogens. OSHA PEL, soluble nickel: 1 mg/m³ TWA (as Ni). Nickel itch is cumulative and permanent once sensitized — minimize skin contact at all times. Air agitation makes nickel mist — local exhaust mandatory. Use PP, CPVC, or lined tanks — plain PVC is marginal above 140°F. Boric acid is a reproductive-hazard concern — avoid dust when charging; gloves; wash after handling. Rectifier = high amperage: LOTO. PPE: goggles + face shield, elbow-length nitrile gloves, apron, boots; P100 or supplied air if ventilation is inadequate.

Operator reference only. Follow your facility SOPs and verify parameters against your chemistry supplier’s TDS and customer specifications. Nickel compounds are classified as suspected carcinogens and known skin sensitizers — consult current SDS. Report any process deviation to your supervisor.

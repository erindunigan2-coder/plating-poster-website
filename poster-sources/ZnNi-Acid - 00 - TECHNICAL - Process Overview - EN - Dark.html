Cathode Efficiency — Nearly Every Amp Deposits Metal
Acid zinc-nickel operates at 90–98% cathode efficiency versus 50–60% for alkaline. Less current is wasted on hydrogen evolution, which means faster plating, lower hydrogen embrittlement risk, and significantly lower energy cost per unit area. Each stage must be controlled independently. Skipping or shortcutting any step compromises the entire deposit — corrosion performance depends on every tank in sequence. Nickel content must stay within 12–15% for gamma-phase alloy formation. Below 12%, the deposit behaves like pure zinc with no corrosion benefit from the nickel addition.

Acid vs. Alkaline — When to Choose Acid
Acid Zn-Ni excels where brightness matters, where barrel plating dominates, and where high throughput justifies the tighter process control.

Alkaline wins where deep-recess throwing power is essential or where ammonium-free wastewater is required.

Bath maintenance: acid systems require closer monitoring of pH, nickel ratio, and brightener levels, but replenishment is simpler — no cyanide-free complexers to rebuild.

Wastewater: acid lines produce ammonium-bearing effluent that many municipal permits restrict. Alkaline avoids this entirely but generates higher volumes of carbonate sludge.
⚠️
ELV Directive & RoHS restrict hexavalent chromium (Cr6+) in passivation. Trivalent Cr3+ is the industry standard for all new applications. Legacy Cr6+ lines are being phased out worldwide.
⚠ Line Safety Overview
NaOH (caustic soda) in cleaning stages — severe chemical burns. HCl in acid activation — ventilate at >1 m/s face velocity. Nickel compounds are Category 1A carcinogens by inhalation — airborne Ni must stay <0.1 mg/m³ (TLV). Chloride mist from the acid bath — corrosive to equipment and lungs. H₂ gas generated at cathode — lower than alkaline but still present; explosive at 4–75% LEL. HE bake oven burns — mandatory bake for high-strength steel (>39 HRC) within 4 hours of plating. PPE required at every tank. Report any chemical spills, unusual odors, or equipment malfunctions to your supervisor immediately. Never work alone on the line during startup or shutdown. Post emergency contact numbers and evacuation routes at both ends of the plating line. Verify fume scrubber operation before starting production each shift.

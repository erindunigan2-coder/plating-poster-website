Routine Transfer Rinse — CN-to-CN Safe Transfer
Immerse parts in flowing ambient water for 30–60 seconds with gentle agitation. This rinse removes copper strike dragout to prevent dilution and contamination of the copper plate bath. Both the preceding and following baths are cyanide-based, so there is no acid/cyanide mixing hazard at this transfer point.

Why This Rinse Matters Despite Compatible Chemistry
Although the copper strike and copper plate baths are both cyanide-based, the strike bath has significantly different concentrations (lower copper, higher free cyanide ratio). Dragging strike solution into the plate bath dilutes it and shifts the copper-to-cyanide ratio, which affects deposit quality, current efficiency, and throwing power. Consistent rinsing between the two baths keeps the plate bath chemistry stable and reduces the frequency of corrective additions.

Safe Transfer Point

This is a cyanide-to-cyanide transfer. Both the preceding bath (the copper strike) and the following bath (the copper plate) are alkaline cyanide solutions. There is no acid/cyanide boundary risk at this rinse station. However, the rinse water itself contains cyanide and must be routed to the cyanide waste treatment system — never to acid waste drains.

Rinse effluent → cyanide destruct system. Never mix with acid waste.

Dragout Reduction

Allow parts to drain over the strike tank for 5–10 seconds before transferring to this rinse. This simple practice reduces chemical carryover by 50–70%, extends rinse water life, reduces waste treatment load, and saves copper cyanide — which is expensive and regulated.

Drain time over strike tank: 5–10 sec before rinse immersion.

Troubleshooting Transfer rinse issues

Plate Bath Chemistry Drifting

Excessive dragout from the strike bath. Increase rinse time. Ensure parts drain over strike tank before transfer. Check flow rate — rinse water may be stagnant or insufficient.

Rinse Water Turning Blue/Green

Abnormal — cyanide copper dragout is colorless to pale straw. A blue/green tint indicates cupric (Cu²⁺) or acid-copper cross-contamination, or an out-of-spec strike bath. Investigate the source and check bath chemistry; do not treat as routine. Route all rinse water to cyanide destruct.

Deposits on Parts After Rinse

Rinse water contaminated or insufficient flow. Check water supply. Parts should exit rinse clean with no visible residue. Increase immersion time if needed.

Staining or Discoloration

Parts sat in rinse too long or rinse water pH too low. Check rinse water pH — should remain alkaline (pH >8) from cyanide carryover. If acidic, investigate source of acid contamination.

⚠ Safety
Rinse water contains cyanide from strike bath dragout. Treat this rinse tank as a cyanide-containing solution. Wear chemical-resistant gloves and face shield. Never route cyanide rinse water to acid waste drains — mixing cyanide with acid generates deadly HCN gas. All effluent from this rinse must go to the cyanide destruct treatment system. Maintain ventilation over the rinse station.

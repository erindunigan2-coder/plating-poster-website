Process Stages — Each One Protects the Next
A decorative chrome line is not just “plate chrome.” It is a precision sequence of cleaning, activation, copper leveling, multi-layer nickel, chromium flash, and post-treatment. Each stage has a specific chemistry, temperature, current density, and dwell time. Deviations propagate forward — a cleaning failure becomes a nickel adhesion failure becomes a chrome peel.

Critical Watch Points Along the Line
Rinses between every process step prevent drag-out contamination — a single rinse failure can crash an entire bath. Water-break test after cleaning is your first go/no-go gate. Temperature and current density at each plating stage must stay within ±2°C and ±0.5 A/dm² of target. The chrome tank is the most sensitive to contamination: 0.2 g/L of Cu or Ni in the chrome bath will darken the deposit.

⚠ Safety Summary — Process Flow
Hex chrome (Cr VI) is an IARC Group 1 carcinogen with an OSHA PEL of 5 µg/m³ as Cr(VI); mist suppressants and local exhaust ventilation are mandatory at the chrome tank. Nickel is a skin sensitizer (GHS Cat. 1), and its soluble forms are a Category 1A carcinogen by inhalation. The acid baths of H₂SO₄ and HCl cause burns, so local exhaust is required. Alkaline cleaners run at pH 12–14 and also burn. Wear a full-face respirator at the chrome tank, keep an emergency shower and eyewash within 10 seconds of all stations, and keep SDSs posted.

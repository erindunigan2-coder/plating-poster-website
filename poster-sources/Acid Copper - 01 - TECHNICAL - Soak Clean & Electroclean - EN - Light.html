Water Break Time
A clean surface holds a continuous, unbroken water sheet. If water beads, organic soil remains — and it will show as pitting, skip plating, or adhesion loss once copper (and its bright leveler) go over it. The test is free and non-negotiable.

The Water-Break Test — Pass vs Fail representative — not to scale

Pass

Continuous Unbroken Film

Continuous unbroken film → clean surface holds water → ready to plate.

Electrocleaning Note
For steel and zinc die cast destined for a strike, electrocleaning after soak is strongly recommended — the soak removes bulk soil, the electroclean strips the microscopic film that causes strike adhesion failure. Cathodic generates ~2× the gas volume for scrubbing but can plate metallic smut; always finish 15–30 s anodic to remove it. On zinc die cast, minimize cathodic time to prevent hydrogen blistering of the casting. For copper/brass substrates going to direct acid copper, a mild anodic or periodic-reverse electroclean avoids smut and etching.

Troubleshooting cleaning stage faults

Water Break Fails

Cause: Low concentration or temp. Fix: Titrate cleaner; increase temp.

Oily Film After Cleaning

Cause: Floating oil redepositing; exhausted cleaner. Fix: Skim/dump; recharge cleaner.

White Residue On Parts

Cause: Hard water salts; heavy drag-out. Fix: Check water quality; improve rinse.

Etching / Discoloration Of Zinc Die Cast

Cause: Cleaner too alkaline/hot for substrate. Fix: Reduce temp; use die-cast-safe cleaner.

Blistering / Adhesion Failure Downstream

Cause: Organic residue not removed. Fix: Add/extend electroclean; verify water break.

Skip Plating (Bare Spots)

Cause: Passive film / fingerprints. Fix: Anodic electroclean finish; clean gloves.

⚠ Safety
Hot alkaline solution — chemical burns on contact. Electrocleaning generates hydrogen and oxygen — ventilation required; no open flames near tanks. Goggles, face shield, elbow-length chemical gloves, apron, chemical-resistant boots. Skin/eye contact: flush 15 min. Electrocleaner carries DC current in solution — LOTO before reaching into the tank.

Technical reference only. Verify all parameters against your chemistry supplier’s TDS, customer specifications, and applicable regulatory requirements before production use. Sulfuric acid causes severe burns; copper sulfate is toxic to aquatic life; cyanide strike chemistries are potentially lethal and must never contact acid.

Why the Strike Exists — Immersion Deposit vs Strike representative — not to scale

Copper is more noble than steel, zinc, and aluminum. Immerse those metals in acid copper and copper deposits by galvanic displacement — loose and non-adherent — before any current is applied. The strike lays down a thin, tightly bonded copper (or nickel) layer so the acid copper builds on a compatible, noble surface.

Critical Process Controls
Thin but complete coverage — the strike must fully cover before entering acid copper; bare spots will immersion-plate.  •  Live entry (“hot entry”) — enter the strike with current already on to avoid a brief immersion deposit at insertion.  •  Guard rinse after the strike — a proper rinse (and often a mild acid dip to remove any strike-surface oxide) precedes acid copper; strike plus rinse together serve as the pre-plate contamination barrier.  •  Bath purity — keep the strike free of building-up substrate metal (iron, zinc); monitor and treat per TDS.

Troubleshooting strike-stage symptoms

Blistering After Acid Copper

Cause: Immersion deposit; incomplete strike coverage; poor activation. Fix: Verify hot entry; extend strike; re-check cleaning / activation.

Strike Does Not Cover Recesses

Cause: Low CD in recess; strike throw too low; short time. Fix: Increase time; improve racking; check bath balance.

Dull / Powdery Strike

Cause: Bath out of balance; contamination. Fix: Analyze / adjust per TDS; carbon treat if organic.

Adhesion Failure at Strike / Basis

Cause: Residual oxide or soil under strike. Fix: Improve clean + activation; hot entry.

Gassy / Dark Strike (Cyanide)

Cause: Low free cyanide; carbonate high. Fix: Adjust free cyanide; decarbonate per TDS.

Even-Handed by Design

Note: Cyanide and alkaline non-cyanide strikes are co-equal options — neither is ranked above the other. Offer both; choose per your shop's chemistry, RoHS posture, and customer specs. Woods nickel and Cu-pyrophosphate serve specialty substrates.

⚠ Safety
CYANIDE STRIKE — LETHAL HAZARD. NEVER allow acid to contact a cyanide bath or cyanide-contaminated parts / rinses — acid + cyanide releases hydrogen cyanide (HCN) gas, which can be fatal. Strict tank segregation, dedicated rinses, and cyanide waste segregation are mandatory. Cyanide antidote protocol and trained response per facility program. Woods nickel strike is strongly acidic and contains nickel (skin sensitizer / presumed human carcinogen, GHS Carc. 1A). Full chemical PPE: goggles + face shield, chemical gloves, apron, boots; local exhaust ventilation. Rectifier = high amperage — LOTO for maintenance. OSHA PEL cyanide (as CN): 5 mg/m³ (skin); OSHA PEL soluble nickel: 1.0 mg/m³ as Ni.

Technical reference only. Verify all parameters against your chemistry supplier’s TDS, customer specifications, and applicable regulatory requirements before production use.

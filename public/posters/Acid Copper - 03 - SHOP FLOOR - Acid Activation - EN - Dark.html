Dip Time — Set by Substrate
The acid removes oxides formed during cleaning and rinsing and leaves a chemically active surface. Under-dip = oxide remains = adhesion failure. Over-dip = etch, smut, and (on high-strength steel) hydrogen loading. Match the time to the metal.

Which Acid?
H₂SO₄ — 5–15% v/v
Preferred on copper lines — adds no chloride to the chain, low fuming. Ambient typical.
HCl — 10–30% v/v
Strong oxide removal — but fumes (ventilation) and adds chloride drag-in. Mind the copper bath.
High-strength steel (>40 HRC): use inhibited acid, minimum time — then bake per ASTM B850. Manual rack agitation only, never air.
✓ Do
  • Match dip time to the substrate — short on brass, copper, zinc
  • Send steel to a strike, never straight to acid copper
  • Use inhibited acid + minimum time on hardened steel
  • Move to rinse promptly — don’t let the surface re-oxidize
✗ Don’t
  • Over-dip brass — it dezincifies and leaves copper smut
  • Air-agitate the acid dip — mist hazard
  • Run spent acid (>~50 g/L Fe) — dump it
  • Add water to acid — always acid to water
⚠ Safety
Strong mineral acid — severe burns and eye damage. HCl fumes at room temperature; hydrogen evolved during pickling is flammable. Goggles + face shield, acid-resistant gloves, apron, boots; respirator if ventilation is inadequate. Always add acid to water when making up.

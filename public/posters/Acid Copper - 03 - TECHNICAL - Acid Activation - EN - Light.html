Substrate-Specific Notes activation varies by base metal

Steel / Iron

Standard H₂SO₄ or HCl activation. Use inhibited acid on >40 HRC steel to limit hydrogen absorption. Steel goes to a strike, never directly to acid copper.

Brass / Copper Alloys

Short dip only (5–15 s). Excess time dezincifies brass and leaves copper smut. A dilute H₂SO₄ activation is gentler. These substrates may proceed to direct acid copper after excellent activation, but a strike improves adhesion.

Zinc Die Cast

Extremely sensitive — mild/proprietary activator, minimal time. Must go to a strike (typically cyanide or alkaline non-cyanide copper).

Aluminum

Requires zincate (zinc immersion) pretreatment, then a strike — not covered by this simple acid dip.

H-Embrittlement Warning
Acid activation is a hydrogen source. For high-strength steel (>40 HRC / >180 ksi UTS), use minimum effective concentration and time, use inhibited acid, and bake per ASTM B850 (375 ±25°F within 4 hours of the last electrodeposition step).

Troubleshooting acid activation issues

Dark Oxide Remains on Steel

Cause: Acid too dilute; time too short. Fix: Increase concentration; extend time (mind H-embrittlement).

Pitting / Etching of Substrate

Cause: Too concentrated/long/warm. Fix: Reduce concentration; shorten dip; add inhibitor.

Adhesion Failure Downstream

Cause: Incomplete activation; residual oxide. Fix: Re-evaluate acid strength/time; check alkaline drag-in.

Copper Smut on Brass

Cause: Dezincification. Fix: Shorten dip; go to copper strike.

Excessive HCl Fuming

Cause: High concentration/temp; poor ventilation. Fix: Reduce to minimum effective; check exhaust.

⚠ Safety
Strong mineral acid — severe burns and eye damage. HCl fumes at room temperature; hydrogen evolved during pickling is flammable. Goggles + face shield, acid-resistant gloves, apron, boots, respirator if ventilation inadequate. Always add acid to water when making up.

What It Is
An acidic bath of copper sulfate (CuSO₄) and sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) with a trace of chloride (Cl⁻) and a suppressor / accelerator / leveler additive package. It deposits soft, ductile, highly leveled copper at near-100% efficiency and room temperature — which is why it undercoats decorative nickel-chrome, fills PCB through-holes and vias, and builds thick electroformed parts.

One Rule You cannot plate acid copper directly on steel, zinc, or aluminum — strike first. Cyanide, alkaline non-cyanide, or Woods nickel; enter live and confirm full coverage. Copper & brass may take direct after excellent activation.

★ Did You Know?
Acid copper is one of the few plating baths that runs at room temperature — no heater on the main tank — while converting roughly 95–100% of the current into metal, which helps keep the deposit low-stress and ductile.

Common Misconceptions what the tank actually does

“It’s just copper sulfate and acid, so it’s easy.”

The metal chemistry is simple — but the additive, chloride, and anode control is precision work. That is where the deposit is won or lost.

“More brightener = brighter.”

Over-addition embrittles and roughens the deposit. Balance the additive package via CVS or a Hull Cell, not by pouring in more.

“Copper is safe.”

Sulfuric acid burns, copper sulfate is highly toxic to aquatic life, and an upstream cyanide strike is potentially lethal.

⚠ Safety Snapshot
Sulfuric acid — burns; always add acid to water. Copper sulfate — aquatic toxicity; waste-treat to permit limits. Acid mist from air agitation — ventilate. Cyanide strike (if used) — never contact acid; segregate the waste stream. Emergency shower & eyewash within reach; current SDS accessible at every station.

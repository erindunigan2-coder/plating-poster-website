Why the Strike Exists — Immersion Happens Before Any Amp Flows
Copper is more noble than steel, zinc, and aluminum. Put those metals in acid copper and copper deposits by galvanic displacement — loose and powdery — before a single amp flows. The strike lays down a thin, tightly bonded flash so acid copper has a compatible base to build on.

RoHS / cyanide-free. Adhesion approaching cyanide with good control. Per supplier TDS.
Specialty routes: Woods nickel — stainless and difficult steels; Copper pyrophosphate — sensitive substrates / plating-on-plastics. Enter every strike live, cover fully, then guard-rinse before acid copper.
✓ Do
  • Enter the strike live (“hot entry”) — current already on
  • Confirm full coverage before acid copper
  • Guard-rinse after the strike to protect the acid bath
  • Keep the strike free of built-up iron / zinc
✗ Don’t
  • Ever let acid touch a cyanide bath, part, or rinse
  • Send active metal to acid copper without a strike
  • Enter dead — a brief immersion deposit forms at insertion
  • Leave recesses uncovered — bare spots immersion-plate
⚠ Safety
CYANIDE STRIKE — LETHAL HAZARD. NEVER allow acid to contact a cyanide bath or cyanide-contaminated parts / rinses — acid + cyanide releases hydrogen cyanide (HCN) gas, which can be fatal. Strict tank segregation, dedicated rinses, and cyanide waste segregation are mandatory; cyanide antidote protocol per facility program. Woods nickel is strongly acidic and contains nickel (skin sensitizer / presumed human carcinogen, GHS Carc. 1A). Full chemical PPE; local exhaust ventilation; rectifier = high amperage — LOTO for maintenance. OSHA PEL cyanide (as CN): [VERIFY]; OSHA PEL soluble nickel: 1.0 mg/m³ as Ni [VERIFY].
Operator reference only. Follow your facility SOPs. Verify parameters against your chemistry supplier’s TDS. Cyanide strike chemistries are potentially lethal and must never contact acid. Report any process deviations to your supervisor.

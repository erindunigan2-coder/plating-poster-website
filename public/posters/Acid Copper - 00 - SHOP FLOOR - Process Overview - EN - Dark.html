8 Stages From Prepared Substrate to Bright, Leveled Copper
Acid copper levels scratches like nothing else — but it plates itself onto bare steel the instant it touches the tank. The strike is not optional.

Process Stages — Clean → Rinse → Activate → Strike → Plate → Rinse → Post-Treat → Final Rinse/Dry
Every stage protects the adhesion chain for the next. Acid copper is the best-leveling common bath — but it will immersion-deposit on any metal more active than copper. Skip the strike and you get blisters, not brightness.
Critical Control Points WATCH THESE 3
01 — CLEANING
Leveled bright copper magnifies every miss. If it’s not water-break clean, nothing downstream can fix it.
04 — STRIKE
The make-or-break layer. No strike on steel/zinc/aluminum = immersion copper and blisters before any amp flows.
05 — PLATING
Chloride, filtration, and anode film control the deposit. Get those three right and the copper is flawless.

✓ Do
  • Strike active metals — steel, zinc die cast, aluminum — every time
  • Water-break test after cleaning; check tank temps at shift start
  • Transfer promptly — never let parts dry between stages
  • Keep fresh copper wet; it tarnishes in minutes
✗ Don’t
  • Put bare steel, zinc, or aluminum straight into acid copper
  • Skip a rinse because the tank “looks clean”
  • Guess at chloride — titrate it (50–80 ppm)
  • Handle fresh copper bare-handed — fingerprints etch in
⚠ Safety — All Stages
Hot caustic (Stage 1) — chemical burns. Mineral acid (Stage 3) — burns and fumes. Copper strike (Stage 4) — if a cyanide strike is used it is potentially lethal; never let acid contact it. Acid copper bath (Stage 5) — sulfuric-acid mist, copper toxic to aquatic life, rectifier arc-flash risk. Wear goggles, face shield, chemical gloves, and apron at all times. Know where the eyewash and shower are before you start. Report spills immediately.

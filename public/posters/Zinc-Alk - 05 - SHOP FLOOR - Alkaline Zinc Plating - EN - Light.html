NaOH : Zn Ratio — The Number That Matters Most
Controls throwing power, brightness, and burning tendency. If you control one number in this tank, control this one. The lab titrates it — you report when something looks off.
What You Must Do
1
Check temperature. Should be 70–90°F. Do not plate if out of range.
2
Load the rack. All parts submerged, good electrical contact at every hook. Verify cathode bar is seated.
3
Set rectifier amperage for the load. Your supervisor will give you the target. Don’t use the same setting for every load.
4
Plate for the required time. Watch voltage/amperage — sudden changes = contact problems.
5
Remove rack. Drain 15–20 sec over the plating tank (drag-out recovery). Transfer to rinse.
✓ Do
  • Report any dark, dull, or rough deposits immediately
  • Adjust rectifier for each load size
  • Drain over the plating tank to recover chemistry
✗ Don’t
  • Use the same rectifier setting for every load
  • Run on “how it looks” — titration tells the truth
  • Add anything to the tank without supervisor approval

Dark streaksIron contamination; anode problemsReport for metal analysis. Check anodes.

⚠ Safety
Hot NaOH (pH >13) — severe burns. H₂ gas at cathode is flammable — no flames within 3 ft. Rectifier = high amperage — shock/arc-flash risk. LOTO for any rectifier work. Goggles + face shield, elbow-length chemical gloves, apron, chemical boots.
Operator reference only. Follow your facility SOPs. Report any process deviations to your supervisor.

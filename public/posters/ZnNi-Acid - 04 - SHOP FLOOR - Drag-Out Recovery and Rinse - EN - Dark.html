<50
µS/cm — Final Rinse Conductivity Before Passivation
Chloride carry-over into trivalent passivation attacks the Cr³⁺ film, causes staining, and shortens bath life. Check conductivity every rack. This number is non-negotiable. Drag-out recovery saves chemistry and reduces waste treatment costs. Every gallon recovered is a gallon not treated and discharged.

Key Procedures — 7 Steps
1
Drip 10–15 s over plating tank at 10–15° angle. Acid drains faster than alkaline.
2
Dip in drag-out tank 20–30 s. This is a still recovery tank, not a working rinse.
3
Pump drag-out back to plating bath when Zn >3 g/L. Check weekly minimum.
4
Run 3-stage counter-flow rinse. DI enters clean stage; water flows opposite to parts.
5
Final rinse <50 µS/cm — mandatory. Chloride destroys Cr³⁺ passivation film.
6
Check final rinse pH 5.0–7.0. Below 4 means acid carry-over — add flow or a stage.
7
Pump back drag-out weekly min. to prevent chloride buildup in the line.

Do best

  • Install conductivity meter with alarm at 75 µS/cm
  • Use 3-stage cascade — chloride is worse than alkaline
  • Angle racks 10–15° during drip time
  • Use DI/RO water for final rinse stage only
  • Return drag-out rinse to the plating bath when concentration allows

Don’t avoid

  • Skip the drag-out tank — acid TDS is high, losses are costly
  • Let chloride-laden water reach passivation
  • Use drag-out as a working rinse with overflow
  • Ignore weekend rinse water — chloride concentrates fast
  • Dump drag-out rinse to drain without waste treatment approval
⚠ Safety — Acid Drag-Out
Drag-out solution is acidic (pH 2–4) with dissolved nickel and zinc chloride. Skin contact causes irritation and burns; eye contact is an emergency. Acid mist from agitation or warm rinse water attacks respiratory tissue — ensure ventilation. Rinse water requires treatment before discharge — dissolved metals exceed POTW limits. Wet floors around rinse tanks — anti-slip footwear and splash goggles required at all times. Drag-out rinse still contains zinc-nickel bath chemistry — treat it as a chemical solution, not clean water. Wear gloves when handling parts.
Operator reference only. Follow your facility SOPs. Report any process deviations to your supervisor.

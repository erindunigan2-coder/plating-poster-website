PAA — The Adhesive Bonding Surface Treatment

The only anodize process designed specifically for structural adhesive bonding — PAA creates a whisker-like porous oxide that mechanical interlocking with adhesive primers achieves peel strengths conventional surfaces cannot match.

Typical Oxide Thickness (µm) — Phosphoric Acid Anodize
PAA produces the thinnest, most open oxide of any anodize process (0.1–0.5 µm). Unlike other anodize types, PAA is never sealed — the open, whisker-like pore structure is the entire point. Adhesive primer wicks into the pores and cures in place, creating a mechanical interlock that resists peel and cleavage forces in bonded airframe assemblies.

Why PAA Creates the Best Bonding Surface
The phosphoric acid electrolyte preferentially dissolves the barrier layer at the pore base while growing new oxide at the metal/oxide interface. This produces an oxide with uniquely open, funnel-shaped pores and finger-like whiskers at the surface. When adhesive primer is applied, it flows into these features and cures around them, creating a mechanical interlock far superior to the smooth, sealed surface of Type II. Boeing testing showed PAA-bonded joints retain >90% of initial peel strength after 14 years of outdoor exposure, compared to <50% for FPL-etch-only bonds.

Did You Know?
PAA was invented by Boeing in the 1970s (U.S. Patent 4,085,012) specifically to improve the durability of adhesively bonded aluminum airframe structures. The wedge crack extension test (ASTM D3762) is the gold standard for evaluating PAA surfaces — a bonded specimen is loaded, exposed to 140°F / 95% RH for 14–28 days, and crack growth is measured. A properly PAA-treated surface shows cohesive failure (adhesive fails, not the interface), proving the bond is stronger than the adhesive itself.

PAA vs FPL Etch for structural bonding

PAA Choose When

  • Structural adhesive bonding (primary)
  • Long-term bond durability required
  • Hot-wet environmental exposure expected
  • Boeing or Airbus bonding spec required
  • Fatigue-critical bonded assemblies

FPL Etch Alone May Suffice When

  • Non-structural or secondary bonding only
  • Short service life or benign environment
  • Field repair with no anodize capability
  • Cost-constrained, non-critical joints
  • Small repair patches (manual application)

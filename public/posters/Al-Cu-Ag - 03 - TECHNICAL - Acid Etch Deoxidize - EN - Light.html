Immersion Time — Short Side for 6xxx Alloys
Over-etching creates a spongy, microporous surface that traps zincate solution in the grain boundaries. This trapped solution outgasses during plating and causes blistering in every layer above. For 6101-T6 and 6061-T6, keep immersion toward the shorter end of the range.

Option A — Nitric / HF Etch

Chemistry: 50% v/v HNO₃ + 3–5% v/v HF (48% conc.)
Temperature: Ambient to 27°C (80°F)
Time: 15–30 seconds
Result: Aggressive, fast oxide removal with micro-roughening. Preferred when surface has heavy oxide or mill scale.

⚠ HF is extremely hazardous. Deep tissue burns may not be immediately painful. Calcium gluconate gel must be at tankside.

Option B — Non-Chrome Deox

Chemistry: Proprietary acid blend per supplier TDS, 15–25% v/v
Temperature: 21–38°C (70–100°F)
Time: 30–60 seconds
Result: Gentler deoxidizing action. Suitable for pre-cleaned extrusions with light native oxide. Longer contact time compensates for lower aggressiveness.

No HF hazard. Rinse water is still acidic — keep away from cyanide zone.

Why This Etch Is Critical
Native Al₂O₃ is electrically insulating (resistivity >10¹⁴ Ω·cm) and reforms within seconds of air exposure. The etch removes this oxide and creates a microscopically roughened surface that the zincate layer can mechanically key into. Without proper etching, zincate adhesion is inconsistent — the zinc deposits preferentially on clean spots and skips oxidized areas, creating localized adhesion failures that propagate through copper and silver layers.

Troubleshooting Etch station issues

Dark Smut After Etch

Alloying elements (Si, Fe, Cu) released as a surface film that the etch has not fully removed. Smut must be cleared before zincate — a de-smut dip (dilute HNO₃) or the etch at proper concentration should handle it. Do not advance parts with visible smut; if persistent, the bath may be exhausted/contaminated — verify alloy cert and bath strength.

Uneven / Patchy Etch

Insufficient cleaning at the preceding clean stage — residual oils or oxides block the acid. Re-clean the part. Also check if etch concentration has dropped below operating range.

Excessive Metal Loss / Pitting

Over-etching: too long, too hot, or HF concentration too high. Reduce immersion time. Verify temperature. Check HF concentration — even 1% overshoot increases etch rate dramatically on 6xxx alloys.

Zincate Won’t Stick (First Zincate Fails)

Parts dried between etch and zincate — oxide reformed. Or under-etching left a passive oxide layer. Re-etch, transfer immediately to rinse, then zincate. No air drying.

⚠ Safety
ACID/CYANIDE INCOMPATIBILITY — acidic dragout or rinse water mixed with the downstream cyanide baths generates lethal HCN gas. Rinse thoroughly and keep all acid carryover out of the cyanide zone. HF (Option A) causes deep tissue burns that may not be immediately painful. Any skin contact — flush with water immediately and apply calcium gluconate gel. Report ALL HF exposures, no matter how minor. Nitric acid is a strong oxidizer — do not store near organics. Both acids generate toxic fumes. Maintain slot ventilation on the etch tank, minimum 100 fpm face velocity. Full-face shield, acid-resistant gloves, apron, and rubber boots required. Bath make-up: ALWAYS add acid to water, never water to acid — adding water to concentrated nitric/HF causes violent localized boiling and hot-acid spattering.

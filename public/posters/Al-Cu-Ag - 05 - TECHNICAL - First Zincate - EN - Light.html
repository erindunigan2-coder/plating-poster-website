Target Zinc Thickness — Thinner Is Better
The zinc layer must be thin, uniform, and fully adherent. A thick or powdery zincate traps solution in the grain boundaries and causes blistering in every layer above. If you cannot see the underlying aluminum grain structure through the zinc, the deposit is too thick. Reduce immersion time.

Displacement Reaction Mechanism
Zincate is an immersion (electroless displacement) process, not an electroplating process. Aluminum is more active than zinc in the EMF series. When bare aluminum contacts the alkaline zincate solution, Al dissolves as aluminate and zinc ions in solution deposit as metallic zinc on the surface: 2Al + 3Zn(OH)₄²⁻ → 2Al(OH)₄⁻ + 3Zn + 4OH⁻. The reaction is self-limiting — once the aluminum surface is fully covered with zinc, the driving force stops. This is why thicker is NOT better: extended immersion produces a loose, powdery outer layer that has no adhesion value.

Good Zincate Appearance

Uniform medium-gray coating across all surfaces. Grain structure of the underlying aluminum may be faintly visible through the zinc. No dark spots (over-deposition). No bare patches (oxide not fully removed). No powdery residue (over-immersion).

Water-break free — rinse water should sheet uniformly off the zincated surface.

Bad Zincate — Stop and Investigate

Dark or black spots indicate contaminated bath (Fe, Cu drag-in) or localized oxide remaining from inadequate etch. Bare areas mean oxide was not removed in the acid etch. Powdery or thick deposit means over-immersion or bath out of balance. Any of these conditions will cause adhesion failure in copper and silver layers above.

If zincate is non-uniform: strip in 50% HNO₃, re-etch, re-zincate. Do not proceed.

Troubleshooting Zincate station issues

Dark or Black Spots

Metallic contamination in zincate bath (iron or copper drag-in from upstream tanks). Carbon-treat the bath. Check rinse integrity between etch and zincate. Verify no cross-contamination from acid zone.

Bare Areas / Incomplete Coverage

Oxide not fully removed in the acid etch. Re-etch the part. Verify etch concentration and immersion time. Check that parts did not dry between etch, rinse, and zincate.

Powdery / Thick Deposit

Over-immersion (too long in the zincate). Reduce time toward 30 seconds. Also check NaOH and ZnO concentrations — high ZnO relative to NaOH accelerates deposition.

Copper or Silver Peels Later

Zincate adhesion failure propagating upward. Verify the double-zincate was performed (first and second zincate, with the nitric strip between them). Check that the first zincate was thin and uniform. Confirm hot entry on the copper strike.

⚠ Safety
Zincate solution is strongly alkaline (NaOH 120–150 g/L, pH >13). Causes severe chemical burns on contact. Wear face shield, chemical-resistant gloves, apron, and rubber boots. If solution contacts skin, flush immediately with water for 15 minutes. Eye contact is a medical emergency — flush with water and seek immediate medical attention. Maintain slot ventilation on the zincate tank.

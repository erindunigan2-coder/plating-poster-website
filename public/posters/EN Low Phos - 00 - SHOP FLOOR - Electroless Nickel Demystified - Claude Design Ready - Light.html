The freshly deposited nickel catalyzes the next cycle. The process is self-sustaining — as long as the bath stays in spec, it keeps plating.

What You Must Do

1

Check temperature and pH on all tanks before starting the shift — a bath out of spec does not deposit well.

2

Follow the 7-stage sequence without skipping: cleaning → rinse → activation → rinse → EN bath → rinse → final treatment.

3

Transfer parts between tanks without letting them air-dry — the activated surface passivates within seconds of air exposure — the activation is lost.

4

Run the water break test after cleaning — if water beads on the part, it is not ready.

5

Report any unusual bath behavior to the shift supervisor immediately — turbidity, foam, strong odor.

Warning Signs

What You See What It Means What to Do
No deposit on parts Activation failed or surface contaminated Notify shift supervisor; review cleaning and activation
Incomplete deposit or spots Rinse issue or bath concentration problem Compare to shift parameter sheet or reference poster; notify supervisor
Cloudy bath, abnormal foam, or strong odor Decomposition — immediate hazard STOP the operation; leave the area immediately; DO NOT attempt to control the bath; call supervisor

Safety — This Line

● Wear splash goggles, chemical-resistant gloves, and apron at all times on the line.
● This line handles acids, caustics, and hot solutions — one mistake can cause severe burns.
● Chemical contact on eyes or skin: remove contaminated clothing immediately, go to the emergency shower or nearest eyewash station and flush for at least 15 minutes — then notify the shift supervisor.
● Emergency shower and eyewash station must be clear and in working condition at all times.
⚠ PHOSPHINE
PH₃
A decomposing bath can release phosphine gas — extremely toxic. If you detect an unusual odor (garlic or fish-like), sudden dizziness, or the bath reacts abnormally: leave the area, do not attempt to control the bath under any circumstances, call the supervisor immediately.

How It Works

1

Adsorption

Hypophosphite adsorbs onto the catalytic metal surface (Ni, Pd-activated, or freshly nickeled steel).

No catalyst, no reaction. Aluminum needs a zincate strike; copper needs a Pd kick-start.

surface| Ni / Pd / Fe
requires| clean & activated
2

Reduction

Hypophosphite (H₂PO₂⁻) gives up two electrons, becomes orthophosphite (HPO₃²⁻) plus atomic hydrogen.

~40% of the reducer ends up as orthophosphite waste — this is what kills bath life.

e⁻ donor| H₂PO₂⁻ → HPO₃²⁻
byproduct| H⁺ + H
3

Deposition

Ni²⁺ accepts the surface electrons and reduces to metallic Ni — codepositing P atoms in the lattice.

Throwing power is uniform: 0.4 mil on a flat face is 0.4 mil inside a blind hole.

rate| 12–25 µm/hr
P codep.| 2–14 wt%
4

Byproduct & Loop

H₂ gas + orthophosphite leave the site; fresh Ni surface re-catalyzes for the next cycle.

Self-sustaining while Ni²⁺, reducer, and complexant stay in spec — stabilizers buy margin, not immunity.

gas| H₂ ↑
limit| ~5 MTO

Decision Rule Need wear? → Low-P. Need everything balanced? → Mid-P. Need corrosion, even if it costs you hardness as-plated? → High-P, then bake.

Bath Control, Failure Modes & Standards

Operating Parameters

Mid-P acid reference bath. Adjust ranges to your supplier's TDS.

Parameter Target Tolerance Effect if Out
Temperature 88 °C ± 2 °C Low: rate crashes. High: bath plate-out, decomposition.
pH 4.7 ± 0.2 Low: rate drops, more P codeposit. High: roughness, runaway.
Ni²⁺ concentration 6.0 g/L ± 0.5 g/L Low: skip plate, slow rate. Replenish before letting it drift.
Reducer (H₂PO₂⁻) 28 g/L ± 3 g/L Low: deposition stalls. High: roughness, gas pitting.
Bath loading 0.5–1.0 dm²/L — Over: starvation, non-uniform thickness. Under: wasted heat.
MTO turnovers ≤ 5 hard limit Past 5: orthophosphite poisoning, dump and recharge.
Filtration 5 µm @ 10×/hr continuous Stop and particles seed plate-out.

MTO Bath-Life Gauge

One MTO = one full turnover of nickel charged. Mid-P bath, acid.

012345+

0–2 MTO

Fresh — full rate, full property spec.

2–4 MTO

Watch — rate drops 10–20%, monitor pH closely.

4–5+ MTO

Dump — orthophosphite saturated, plate-out risk.

Pitting

Gas adhering to horizontal faces. Increase agitation, re-rack.

Skip Plate

Activation failed — Pd missed, oxide returned. Re-strike.

Roughness

Particulate seeding plate-out. Filter, check tank walls.

Blistering

Substrate not clean / activated. Adhesion lost — strip and redo prep.

Plate-Out

Bath decomposition. Stabilizer low or pH high.

Edge Pull-Back

Excessive stabilizer. Re-rack, dose carefully.

Governing Standards

Cite the spec on the print. The bath chemistry follows from the class & service condition.

AMS 2404 / 2405
Aerospace plating specs2404 = electroless Ni-P, classes 1–4 by thickness. 2405 = electroless Ni-B (boron-reduced).
ASTM B733
Industrial Ni-P, autocatalyticU.S. workhorse spec. Type I–V (P content), SC1–SC4 (service), T1–T7 (thickness).

International equivalent of B733Used outside North America. Same intent; codes differ.
MIL-C-26074E
U.S. military, supersededReplaced by SAE-AMS-C-26074. Drawings still reference it.

Salt spray & XRF thicknessB117 proves corrosion claims; B568 is the standard for non-destructive XRF thickness measurement.

Where It Lives

Oil & Gas

Valves, downhole tools, sour service — High-P.

Aerospace

Hydraulic actuators, landing gear — AMS 2404.

Electronics

Connectors, heat sinks, EMI — Low-P.

Food & Pharma

Valves, fittings, mold release — High-P.

Automotive

Fuel system, brake, drivetrain — Mid-P.

Mold & Die

Plastics tooling, release surfaces — Mid-P + bake.

Bath Decomposition / Phosphine

A bath upset — pH below 4, particulate seeding, or cooling failure — can run away. In extreme upset, hypophosphite + heat can evolve phosphine (PH₃), fatally toxic.

Vent & evacuate first. Never neutralize a runaway in place — pump to a passivated quench tank.

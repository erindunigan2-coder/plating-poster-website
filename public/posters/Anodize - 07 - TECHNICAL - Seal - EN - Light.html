Close the Pores — Lock In Color, Corrosion Resistance, and Hardness

An unsealed anodize is an unfinished anodize. The seal converts porous oxide to a dense, hydrated barrier.

Hot Water Seal — The Gold Standard
Boiling or near-boiling DI water hydrates the porous Al&sub2;O&sub3; into boehmite (AlOOH), swelling the pore walls shut. Nickel acetate and mid-temp seals offer alternatives with different trade-offs. The seal step locks in dye color, closes the porous oxide structure, and provides the final corrosion resistance. An incomplete seal is the single most common cause of field failures in anodized parts — always verify with a dye-spot test (ASTM B136) or admittance measurement.

Key Procedures 5 steps

1
Verify seal bath: temperature, pH, and chemistry (Ni concentration if using nickel acetate). Use a calibrated thermometer and pH meter — do not rely on controller readouts alone.
2
Transfer from dye (or rinse) immediately. Do not let parts cool or dry — open pores exposed to air will absorb contaminants and produce hazy or blotchy seals.
3
Immerse fully. Ensure all surfaces are submerged and rack parts to allow free circulation. Air pockets prevent sealing and cause localized corrosion failures.
4
Hold for required time: 2–3 min/µm of oxide thickness for hot water; follow supplier TDS for Ni acetate. Thicker coatings (hard anodize) need proportionally longer seal times.
5
Remove and dry. Verify seal quality with dye-spot test (ASTM B136) or admittance test. A well-sealed surface will resist dye penetration within 5 minutes of application.

Troubleshooting

Failed Dye-Spot Test

Incomplete seal. Temp too low, time too short, or pH wrong. Verify temp at 200°F+ with calibrated probe; extend immersion time; check and adjust pH to 5.5–6.5.

White Powder / Smut

Ni acetate pH too high or concentration too high. Lower pH to 5.5; reduce Ni concentration; improve post-seal rinse to remove residual salts.

Color Bleed / Fade

Seal pH too low (acid leaching dye out of pores) or temperature ramped too fast. Raise pH above 5.5; ramp temperature gradually; verify dye fixation before sealing.

Soft / Chalky Surface

Over-seal (too long at temperature) or DI water contaminated with dissolved solids. Check water quality and conductivity; reduce seal time; replace bath if TDS is high.

Do best

  • Use DI water for all seal tanks
  • Monitor pH with calibrated meter
  • Verify seal with dye-spot test (ASTM B136)
  • Keep seal tank covered to reduce evaporation
  • Perform dye-spot test (ASTM B136) on sealed parts from every batch

Don’t avoid

  • Use tap water (minerals cause smut/haze)
  • Skip seal quality testing
  • Over-seal (damages oxide structure)
  • Let seal tank go below temp between loads
  • Load cold parts directly into boiling seal tank — thermal shock cracks oxide
⚠ Safety — Boiling Water + Steam + Nickel
Hot water seal operates at or near boiling (200–212°F). Steam and splash cause instant burns. Nickel acetate is a skin sensitizer — repeated exposure causes allergic contact dermatitis. PPE: heat-rated chemical gloves, face shield, apron, sleeve guards. Steam ventilation required. Keep tank covered between loads. Nickel-containing wastewater requires treatment before discharge. Seal tanks operate at near-boiling temperatures. Steam burns can cause severe injuries. Stand to the side when loading — never reach over an open hot seal tank. Steam inhalation can scald airways — ensure overhead ventilation is operating before opening the tank. If using nickel acetate, Ni-containing wastewater must be treated to below 1 mg/L before discharge per local permit limits.

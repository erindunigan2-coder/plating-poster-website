Typical Chrome Thickness — The Thinnest Layer Does the Most Visible Work
The chromium flash is only 0.25–0.75 µm (10–30 µin) — blue-white mirror finish and tarnish resistance. Corrosion protection comes from 20–40 µm of duplex or triplex nickel below.

Why the Nickel System Matters More Than the Chrome
Bright Ni (with sulfur) is anodic to semi-bright Ni, so corrosion spreads laterally instead of penetrating to the substrate. Microporous Ni distributes >10,000 corrosion cells/cm² across the surface. Semi-bright Ni must be ≥60% of total Ni thickness to maintain the STEP test ΔE of 100–200 mV. Chrome is purely cosmetic — the nickel architecture determines part life.

Did You Know?
The first decorative chromium electroplate was achieved in 1924 by Colin Fink and Charles Eldridge at Columbia University. Within a decade, chrome-plated bumpers defined the American automobile. The paradox: the entire chrome layer on a car bumper is thinner than a human red blood cell (~7 µm), yet it provides the iconic mirror finish. The real engineering is in the nickel underneath.

Hex Chrome vs Tri Chrome positioning

Hex Chrome Strengths Legacy

  • Blue-white mirror finish — industry benchmark for 80+ years
  • Wider operating window; easier to control in production
  • More forgiving of current density variations on complex parts

Tri Chrome Advantages Future

  • Dramatically lower toxicity — no Cr(VI) in the bath
  • Easier regulatory compliance (REACH, EPA, OSHA PEL)
  • Lower waste treatment cost; no hex-to-tri reduction step
